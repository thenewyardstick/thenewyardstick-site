Volkswagen Guide

TNY 17 Jul 2026

Volkswagen continues to offer one of the broadest model ranges on sale, covering everything from compact hatchbacks to seven-seat SUVs, electric MPVs and pick-ups. Electrification is now spreading across almost every corner of the line-up, with EVs and plug-in hybrids sitting alongside petrol and diesel models, allowing buyers to choose the powertrain that best suits their needs.

Comfortable cabins, intuitive technology and everyday practicality remain key strengths, while distinctive models such as the T-Roc Cabriolet prove Volkswagen can still inject a little fun into its range. Partnerships with Ford on commercial vehicles and selected electric models also allow Volkswagen to focus investment where it matters most, helping strengthen one of the industry's most comprehensive line-ups.

Current Models

Comfortable, refined and easy to drive, it remains a strong all-rounder, although its 381-litre boot is now smaller than many family hatchback rivals.

Solid driving dynamics and excellent everyday usability make it an ideal small SUV for smaller families, while its practicality helps keep it competitive in an increasingly crowded segment.

Increased dimensions provide more passenger space and a larger boot, while mild-hybrid powertrains and improved refinement make Volkswagen’s popular compact SUV a more complete and grown-up family car.

Dimensions : 4271 × 1811 × 1522 mm

Key rivals : BMW 2 Series Convertible, Honda Prelude, Mazda MX-5, MINI Convertible, MG Cyberster 

The T-Roc Cabriolet is the fun option in Volkswagen’s range, taking the unusual convertible-SUV formula pioneered by the Range Rover Evoque Convertible and offering it in a more accessible, value-focused package. With four seats, familiar Volkswagen mechanicals and a fully electric fabric roof, it remains a genuinely distinctive choice while it is still available new.

Volkswagen Passat (24-)

RRP From : £40,875
Pre Reg From : c. £31,500
Used Price From : c. £22,000

Dimensions : 4917 × 1849 × 1506 mm

Key rivals : BYD Seal 6, Jaecoo 8, MG S9, Peugeot 5008, Skoda Superb

The Passat is a refreshingly traditional alternative to the increasingly SUV-heavy large-car market. Closely related to the Skoda Superb, it offers excellent passenger space, a large boot and strong long-distance comfort. Those seeking maximum versatility and space may still prefer a large SUV, but for families who don’t want one, the Passat remains a practical choice.

Volkswagen ID.7 (23-)

Powertrains : EV
Dimensions : 4961 × 1862 × 1536 mm
Boot : 532L

Key rivals : BYD Seal, DS No8, Hyundai Ioniq 6, Mazda 6e, Volvo ES90

The ID.7 is Volkswagen’s large electric alternative to the Passat, combining excellent refinement and generous interior space. Its high list prices can be difficult to justify alongside the Passat, but substantial pre-registered discounts change the picture considerably. Used examples can be particularly tempting, with heavy depreciation making the ID.7 a lot of car for the money.

Volkswagen Caddy (20-)

RRP From : £31,950
Pre Reg From : c. £31,000
Used Price From : c. £15,000

Powertrains : Diesel, Petrol
Dimensions : 4500 / 4853 × 1855 × 1833 mm

Key rivals : Ford Tourneo Connect, Mercedes-Benz Citan Tourer, Peugeot Rifter, Renault Kangoo, Toyota Proace City Verso

The Caddy shares much of its engineering with the Ford Transit Connect, helping Volkswagen reduce development costs while retaining its own character. Van-based origins provide huge amounts of passenger and luggage space, making it a particularly practical family car. Inside, considerably more technology than previous generations gives the latest Caddy a more modern, car-like feel.

Volkswagen Caravelle (22-)

Used Price From : c. £32,000

Dimensions : 4973 × 1941 × 1907 mm
Boot : 469L

Key rivals : Ford Tourneo Custom, Kia PV5, Maxus Mifa 9, Toyota Proace Verso, Vauxhall Vivaro Life

The Caravelle is another product of Volkswagen’s collaboration with Ford, sharing its foundations with the latest Transit Custom. A recent reshuffling of Volkswagen’s van-based passenger models may confuse some buyers, but this is effectively the newest generation of its traditional van-based people mover. Huge interior space and flexible seating make it particularly well suited to larger families and passenger transport.

Volkswagen ID.Buzz (22-)

The ID. Buzz takes a more style-led approach than most electric van-based MPVs, sacrificing some outright space for a distinctive design that also helps improve efficiency and electric range compared with boxier rivals. Short- and long-wheelbase versions provide plenty of versatility, while substantial depreciation means used examples can offer particularly strong value compared with their relatively high new prices.

Volkswagen Tiguan (24-)

 RRP From : £39,985
Pre Reg From : c. £29,000
Used Price From : c. £22,500

Powertrains : MHEV, PHEV, Diesel, Petrol
Dimensions : 4539 × 1842 × 1658 mm
Boot : 652L

Key rivals : Hyundai Tucson, Kia Sportage, Peugeot 3008, Renault Austral, Toyota RAV4

The Tiguan is a practical family SUV with plenty of passenger space, a useful boot and a technology-focused interior centred around a large touchscreen. A broad choice of petrol, diesel and plug-in hybrid powertrains adds to its versatility, although prices have climbed considerably and higher-spec versions can become expensive. It remains a well-rounded choice for everyday family use.

Volkswagen Tayron (25-)

RRP From : £41,635
Pre Reg From : c. £34,500
Used Price From : c. £28,000

Powertrains : MHEV, PHEV, Diesel, Petrol
Dimensions : 4792 × 1853 × 1666 mm
Boot : 652L

Key rivals : Chery Tiggo 9, Hyundai Santa Fe, Jaecoo 8, Mazda CX-80, Peugeot 5008

The Tayron replaces the Tiguan Allspace and takes Volkswagen into the increasingly competitive three-row SUV market. It follows much the same philosophy as the Tiguan, with a technology-led interior, broad powertrain choice and family-focused practicality, but stretches the formula into a longer body with space for up to seven. It provides a straightforward step up for families needing more room.

Volkswagen ID.4 (21-)

RRP From : £36,995
Pre Reg From : c. £26,500
Used Price From : c. £9,500

Powertrains : EV
Dimensions : 4584 × 1852 × 1634 mm
Boot : 543L

Key rivals : Citroën ë-C5 Aircross, Jeep Compass, Leapmotor B10, Nissan Ariya, Renault Scenic E-Tech

The ID.4 is Volkswagen’s electric alternative to the Tiguan, combining an aerodynamic body with a spacious, technology-led cabin and useful family practicality. A growing number of newer rivals now offer greater range at lower prices, increasing the competition around it, but the ID.4 remains a strong all-round option, particularly on the increasingly affordable used market.

Volkswagen ID.5 (22-)

RRP From : £36,995
Pre Reg From : c. £29,500
Used Price From : c. £15,500

Powertrains : EV
Dimensions : 4589 × 1852 × 1615 mm
Boot : 549L

Key rivals : Ford Capri, Hyundai Ioniq 5, Nissan Ariya, Peugeot E-3008, Toyota bZ4X

The ID.5 is the more style-led alternative to the ID.4, adding extra character with its coupé-SUV body without sacrificing much of the practicality underneath. The sleeker shape also brings a small improvement in aerodynamic efficiency and range, while pricing remains close to the ID.4. It’s an appealing option for buyers wanting something a little more distinctive.

Volkswagen Amarok (23-)

RRP From : £47,940
Pre Reg From : c. £45,000
Used Price From : c. £24,000

Powertrains : Diesel
Dimensions : 5350 × 1910 × 1888 mm
Boot : Pickup

Key rivals : Ford Ranger, Isuzu D-Max, KGM Musso, Maxus T90 EV, Toyota Hilux

The Amarok is closely related to the Ford Ranger, combining proven pick-up foundations with Volkswagen’s own styling and interior treatment. Buyers wanting a plug-in hybrid will need to choose the Ford for now, while a growing number of new rivals are increasing competition. Even so, the Amarok remains a strong workhorse, with substantial towing capability and a generous payload capacity.
