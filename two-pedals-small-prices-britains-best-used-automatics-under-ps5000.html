Two Pedals, Small Prices – Britain's Best Used Automatics Under £5,000

📈 Market Watch 26 Nov 2026

Automatic cars have become increasingly popular, but buying a new one can still be an expensive exercise.

Fortunately, the second-hand market offers some surprisingly appealing alternatives, with £5,000 opening the door to everything from premium hatchbacks to electric cars, performance models and comfortable family estates.

We've searched the market for ten interesting options that demonstrate just how much choice is available. Some prioritise economy, others offer space or driving enjoyment, while a few are simply unusual alternatives to the conventional choices.

All have one thing in common: they bring automatic motoring within relatively easy reach.


A 2013 BMW 120d is an interesting starting point for anyone wanting something a little more premium. Examples with fewer than 90,000 miles can be found within our £5,000 budget, offering a rear-wheel-drive layout, a strong diesel engine and the sort of driving experience that helped establish BMW's reputation.

The 1 Series isn't the most spacious car in its class, with its rear-wheel-drive architecture compromising some interior packaging compared with front-wheel-drive alternatives. However, this is a trade-off many enthusiastic drivers will happily accept. There's enough room for a small family, while the 120d offers plenty of performance and respectable fuel economy.

It's an appealing combination of premium character and everyday usability, though buyers should factor in the well-known N47 timing chain risk—mounting the chain at the rear against the gearbox makes replacement labour-heavy and costly if wear or extended oil intervals go unchecked.


The Citroën C4 Cactus is one of the most distinctive cars in its class, and the 1.6 BlueHDi offers an impressive combination of economy, comfort and practicality. Around £5,000 can secure a 2016 example with approximately 60,000 miles, making it an interesting proposition for buyers who want something different.

The Cactus has a surprisingly spacious interior, comfortable suspension and plenty of character. Its unusual Airbump exterior panels and minimalist dashboard help it stand apart from more conventional family hatchbacks.

The diesel engine is economical without feeling particularly underpowered, while the 1.2-litre petrol is another tempting option for lower-mileage motorists.

However, PureTech petrol buyers should pay particular attention to the timing belt, which runs in oil and requires careful maintenance. On the diesel automatic, buyers should also understand that the early ETG transmission is an automated manual rather than a conventional torque-converter automatic, so a thorough test drive is essential.


Few small cars have the same personality as the Fiat 500. Its retro-inspired styling, compact dimensions and distinctive interior make it a familiar choice for urban motorists, while the 1.2-litre petrol engine offers modest running costs.

Around £5,000 can secure a 2012 example with approximately 60,000 miles. The 0.9-litre TwinAir is another interesting option, offering a more characterful driving experience and a little extra performance, although its real-world economy depends heavily on driving style.

Those needing more practicality should also consider the Fiat Panda. It offers a roomier and more versatile interior without taking up substantially more space on the road. Both models have their own charm, making them attractive alternatives to more conventional city cars.

As with many small cars of this era, buyers should remember that some Fiat 500 automatics use an automated-manual transmission, making a test drive especially worthwhile.


The Honda Jazz has long enjoyed a reputation for exceptional reliability, and the used market offers plenty of choice for buyers with £5,000 to spend. A search brings up numerous 2010–2014 examples, some with as little as 50,000 miles.

Its clever interior packaging provides impressive passenger space for such a compact car, while the flexible seating arrangement makes it particularly useful for small families. Buyers who need something more substantial than a Fiat 500 or Panda should certainly consider one.

There are also hybrid examples available within budget, often with around 70,000 miles. These offer an interesting alternative for urban motorists, combining Honda's petrol-electric technology with the Jazz's practical design.

However, buyers should pay attention to the gearbox fitted. Earlier petrol automatics used Honda's i-SHIFT automated manual transmission, while later petrol models and the Jazz Hybrid used CVT technology. These offer quite different driving characteristics, so buyers should identify the specific transmission and check its maintenance history before purchasing.



The Kia Optima is an overlooked alternative to established family-car favourites such as the Ford Mondeo. Its attractive styling, generous equipment and comfortable interior make it an interesting yet useful used purchase.

The 1.7-litre CRDi diesel offers a good balance of economy and performance, with sufficient torque to move the substantial saloon without feeling underpowered. A 2015 example with around 90,000 miles can fall within our budget, while many comparable rivals command considerably more money.

The estate version is particularly tempting, offering a substantial amount of luggage space and making the Optima a genuine alternative for families who need more room. Its relative lack of popularity on the used market works in buyers' favour, potentially securing a lot of car for relatively little money.


Not every affordable automatic needs to prioritise economy. The Renault Clio Renaultsport 200 offers something rather different, combining hot-hatch performance with an automatic dual-clutch transmission.

A £5,000 budget can bring a 2014 example with approximately 90,000 miles into consideration. Its 1.6-litre turbocharged petrol engine delivers strong performance, while the EDC gearbox provides quick shifts without requiring the driver to operate a clutch pedal.

Tyres, brakes and suspension components can prove more expensive than those fitted to a standard Clio, so buyers should budget accordingly.

Despite its performance credentials, the Clio can still be used as an everyday car, offering reasonable practicality and the potential to put a smile on the driver's face. There are also more economical diesel Clio automatics available for buyers who prioritise low running costs, with useful low-down torque making them particularly suitable for everyday driving.


A £5,000 budget doesn't restrict buyers to conventional petrol and diesel automatics. The Renault Zoe brings fully electric motoring into the equation, and the 41kWh ZE40 version is particularly interesting.

A 2018 example with approximately 90,000 miles can fall within budget, offering an official range of up to 186 miles when new, although battery condition and weather can significantly affect real-world performance.

With no conventional gearbox to operate, the Zoe provides effortless urban driving and potentially very low running costs. However, buyers must establish whether the particular vehicle has an owned battery or a battery-leasing arrangement. This can make a substantial difference to the overall cost of ownership.


For motorists who need substantial interior space and all-wheel drive, the Subaru Outback deserves considerably more attention than it typically receives.

The 2.5-litre Boxer petrol CVT pairs Subaru's horizontally opposed engine layout with its Symmetrical AWD system, trading low-end diesel torque for smoother refinement and fewer emissions headaches. Producing around 165–170PS and roughly 229Nm of torque, it favours linear, rev-happy progress over diesel grunt, while the Lineartronic transmission keeps cruising relaxed—though it is noticeably thirstier in daily driving than the 2.0D.

Its estate body delivers generous passenger space and practical load-lugging ability, while raised ground clearance and protective cladding bring SUV-style versatility without bulkier crossover proportions. For buyers who want an all-weather estate rather than another SUV, it remains a credible, low-key alternative.

At around 110,000 miles on a 2011 example, coming in £1,000 under budget gives a useful financial buffer against age-related wear and higher fuel bills. Buyers should check oil consumption and top-up history, evidence of Lineartronic servicing, timing belt/water pump records (where applicable on the earlier 2.5 setup), and suspension or underbody condition before committing.


The Vauxhall Ampera was a brave move by General Motors, bringing extended-range electric motoring to British buyers years before the technology became more widely discussed.

Launched in 2012, it was the first range-extender electric car from a European manufacturer. Its innovative drivetrain uses an electric motor to drive the wheels, with a 1.4-litre petrol engine acting as a generator when the battery charge becomes depleted.

For shorter journeys, owners with access to home charging can potentially complete much of their everyday driving without using petrol. When longer journeys are required, the petrol range extender removes much of the range anxiety associated with early electric vehicles.

Its futuristic interior and exterior still attract attention, while the electric motor delivers brisk acceleration and smooth performance. A 2013 example with approximately 80,000 miles can fall within our £5,000 budget.

There are compromises, however. The Ampera has just four seats, and its relatively unusual technology means buyers should seek specialist knowledge and carefully inspect the battery, charging system and range-extender operation. Nevertheless, it is one of the most interesting cars available at this price.


When considering an affordable Volkswagen automatic, the Golf is perhaps the obvious choice. However, the Beetle offers something a little more individual.

The modern Beetle combines recognisable styling with a surprisingly sophisticated interior in the right specification. It shares much of its mechanical architecture with other Volkswagen Group products, meaning buyers have a familiar range of engines and transmissions to consider.

We would be inclined to investigate the 2.0-litre diesel rather than automatically choosing the smaller 1.2-litre petrol, although condition and maintenance history should take priority over engine size. DSG-equipped examples offer convenient automatic motoring, but buyers should check for smooth operation, evidence of the correct transmission servicing and any signs of fluid leaks.

For motorists who want something more distinctive than another Golf, the Beetle makes a compelling alternative without sacrificing everyday usability.

— The used market demonstrates that £5,000 can secure considerably more than a basic city car with an automatic gearbox. Whether it's the rear-wheel-drive appeal of a BMW 120d, the practicality of a Honda Jazz, the performance of a Clio Renaultsport or the technological curiosity of a Vauxhall Ampera, there is something for a surprisingly wide range of motorists.

The important thing is to buy carefully. Automatic transmissions vary considerably in design and complexity, and some of the cars featured here require more specialist attention than others. Service records, transmission condition and a comprehensive inspection should all form part of the buying process.

For those prepared to look beyond the obvious choices, two pedals and a small budget can make for a surprisingly interesting combination.
