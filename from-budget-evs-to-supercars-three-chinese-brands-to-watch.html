From Budget EVs to Supercars: Three Chinese Brands to Watch

📝 Articles 29 Sep 2026

Chinese carmakers are no longer simply arriving in Europe with a handful of cheap EVs. They are building factories, expanding dealer networks, developing premium sub-brands and, in some cases, moving faster than many established manufacturers can comfortably match.

Three names in particular show just how varied that expansion has become. BYD brings enormous scale, battery expertise and an increasingly broad range stretching from affordable hatchbacks to Yangwang hypercars. Leapmotor is growing at remarkable speed with Stellantis behind its international push, while MG has transformed from a struggling British marque into one of China’s most established automotive exports.

BYD has grown from a small Shenzhen battery maker, founded in 1995, into one of the world’s largest car manufacturers. It entered the automotive industry in 2003, and its expertise in batteries remains central to cars such as the Dolphin, Seal and Seal U.

In 2025, BYD sold around 4.6 million new-energy vehicles and generated roughly RMB 804 billion (£84bn) in revenue, while its 17-millionth NEV was produced in July 2026.

Europe is now a major focus. BYD’s first European passenger-car factory in Szeged, Hungary, is due to begin production in late 2026, initially building the Dolphin Surf. The company is already looking for a second European assembly site, as it shifts from being primarily a Chinese exporter towards a genuinely global manufacturer. 

The Dolphin Surf is the entry point to BYD’s UK range and a direct rival for cars such as the Fiat Grande Panda. At just under four metres long, the cheapest Active version uses a 30kWh Blade Battery, an 87bhp motor and offers 137 miles of WLTP range, with 65kW rapid charging.

Its £18,675 list price is already competitive, but lightly used and pre-registered examples have appeared for under £14,000, making it particularly interesting on value.


The BYD Seal is probably the clearest proof that the brand can do more than value-focused EVs. It is a genuine Tesla Model 3 rival, with sleek proportions, a notably high-quality interior and plenty of performance.

The rear-wheel-drive Design model uses an 82.5kWh Blade Battery, produces 308bhp, reaches 62mph in 5.9 seconds and offers up to 354 miles of WLTP range. Step up to the Excellence AWD and power jumps to 523bhp, cutting 0–62mph to 3.8 seconds, while range remains a useful 323 miles.

Both versions are generously equipped, with features such as a panoramic roof, heat pump, heated and ventilated front seats and a substantial suite of driver-assistance technology. Combined with the Seal’s clean cabin design and solid material quality, that helps it feel much closer to an established premium saloon than a newcomer.


But BYD’s ambitions extend well beyond its mainstream range. Sitting above the core brand is Denza, positioned as a more premium, technology-led marque, while Yangwang occupies the very top of the group as its ultra-luxury and high-performance division.

Denza is already expanding into Europe, while Yangwang remains more exclusive, acting as a showcase for some of BYD’s most advanced engineering. Together, they give BYD a route from affordable hatchbacks right through to six-figure luxury cars and hypercars.

The Yangwang U9 Xtreme is unlikely to trouble UK order books, but it shows just how far BYD’s engineering ambitions now stretch. Based on the U9 hypercar, the Xtreme uses a 1,200V electrical architecture and four independent motors producing more than 3,000PS, with BYD limiting production to just 30 cars.

The Yangwang U8 shows how far BYD could eventually push into the top end of the UK market. Positioned as a Range Rover rival, the huge luxury SUV uses a range-extender powertrain with four electric motors producing around 1,180bhp and 1,280Nm, while independently controlled wheels enable its dramatic tank turn. It can even float briefly in an emergency, underlining Yangwang’s technology-first approach.

UK sales are not yet confirmed, but BYD brought the larger U8L to the 2026 Goodwood Festival of Speed alongside the U9 Xtreme and U7, complete with a dedicated Yangwang VIP area. It suggests BYD is already testing how the marque could sit above Denza in Britain.

Leapmotor is one of China’s younger carmakers, founded in Hangzhou in 2015, but its expansion has accelerated dramatically since Stellantis invested €1.5 billion and became its largest shareholder. Their Stellantis-led Leapmotor International joint venture now handles sales and manufacturing outside Greater China, giving the brand access to an established global dealer and production network.

That footprint is already growing: the C10 and B10 are now assembled in Gurun, Malaysia, while European production plans include the B10 at Zaragoza, Spain, with further Leapmotor models intended for Stellantis’ Madrid plant.

While many Chinese brands have concentrated their European launches around SUVs, Leapmotor also arrived with something much smaller. The T03 city car gives the brand an unusually accessible entry point, combining compact dimensions with equipment more commonly associated with larger cars.

Standard kit includes a 10.1-inch touchscreen, panoramic glass roof, climate control, reversing camera and a broad suite of driver-assistance systems. Its 37.3kWh LFP battery is another strong point. Lithium iron phosphate chemistry is valued for durability, thermal stability and strong cycle life, making it well suited to an everyday EV. It provides up to 165 miles of WLTP range, while the motor produces 94bhp.

The Leapmotor B05 is where the brand starts to look much more ambitious. Rather than another crossover, it is a sleek C-segment electric hatchback that could appeal to buyers who might otherwise consider premium hatchbacks such as the BMW 1 Series or Mercedes-Benz A-Class, but want an EV instead.

It uses a 67.1kWh LFP battery, offers up to 300 miles of WLTP range, and sends 215bhp to the rear wheels. Launch Control helps it reach 62mph in 6.7 seconds, while 174kW rapid charging can add around 150 miles of WLTP-rated range in 17 minutes.

The Leapmotor B10 takes the brand’s value-led approach into the family SUV market. At 4,515mm long, it is roughly BMW iX1-sized, while prices start at just £29,995.

Standard equipment is generous, including a panoramic roof, heated and ventilated electric front seats, heated steering wheel, 360-degree camera, 12-speaker audio system, heat pump and V2L capability. The pure-electric version uses a 67.1kWh LFP battery, offers up to 270 miles of WLTP range and produces 215bhp.

More unusual is the UK-available REEV, which can travel around 50 miles on battery power alone — enough to cover many daily journeys without using petrol. On longer trips, a 1.5-litre petrol engine acts purely as a generator, extending combined range to around 559 miles while the wheels remain electrically driven.

The Leapmotor C10 is currently the brand’s European halo model, and it has the size and road presence to match. At 4.74 metres long with a 2.83-metre wheelbase, it offers genuinely generous rear-seat space and feels like a much larger, more substantial car than its price suggests.

Buyers can choose between a pure-electric version with up to 263 miles of WLTP range or the REEV, which combines a 28.4kWh battery with a 1.5-litre petrol generator for 94 miles of electric-only range and 602 miles overall.

New prices start from £32,750, but pre-registered examples are now appearing around the £27,000 mark. For something this large, spacious and well equipped, that is an enormous amount of car for the money.

MG has one of the most complicated histories of any major car brand. Founded in Britain in the 1920s, it passed through a string of owners, restructurings and near-death experiences before MG Rover finally collapsed in 2005. The MG name survived, first under Nanjing Automobile and then SAIC Motor, which has transformed it from a struggling British marque into a major global brand.

The MG3 remains one of the clearest examples of MG’s value-first approach. The latest model is a properly modern supermini, with a 10.25-inch central display, 7-inch digital driver screen, reversing camera, Apple CarPlay/Android Auto and MG Pilot safety systems all available from the lower end of the range.

The regular petrol version uses a 1.5-litre four-cylinder producing around 113bhp, while the Hybrid+ combines a 1.5-litre engine with a much stronger electric motor for around 192bhp combined, 0–62mph in 8.0 seconds and economy of roughly 64mpg.


The MG4 XPOWER is where MG’s value argument becomes almost absurd. Its dual-motor, all-wheel-drive setup produces around 429bhp and 600Nm, launching the family hatchback from 0–62mph in just 3.8 seconds — fractionally quicker than a current Mercedes-AMG A45 S.

It still uses a practical five-door hatchback body and a 64kWh battery, with official range around 239 miles.

What really makes it stand out is price. New XPOWERs now list from around £33,995, while pre-registered cars can be found comfortably below £30,000. That puts genuine super-hatch performance into a car costing roughly half as much as an A45 S, which starts at around £65,000.

The MG S9 PHEV takes MG’s value formula into the full-size seven-seat SUV market. At 4.98 metres long and 1.96 metres wide, with a 2.92-metre wheelbase, it is a genuinely substantial family car, offering 332 litres of boot space with all seven seats in use and a huge 1,026 litres with the third row folded.

Its plug-in hybrid system combines a 1.5-litre turbo petrol engine with a 24.7kWh battery, producing around 295bhp and offering up to 62 miles of electric-only range. It can also tow up to 2,000kg. Standard equipment includes a panoramic roof, heated front seats, tri-zone climate control, 360-degree camera and electric tailgate.

The MG Cyberster proves that MG is not limited to sensible, value-focused family cars. The two-seat electric roadster brings back some of the brand’s sporting character, but with thoroughly modern hardware: scissor doors, a 77kWh battery, and up to 496bhp and 725Nm in the dual-motor GT. That version reaches 62mph in just 3.2 seconds, while the rear-wheel-drive Trophy offers up to 316 miles of range.

At £55,245, the Cyberster is expensive by MG standards, but still strong value for something with this level of performance, drama and rarity.

That breadth is what makes modern MG so interesting. From the £13,500 MG3 and 429bhp MG4 XPOWER to seven-seat SUVs and an electric roadster, it has managed to keep value at the centre of the brand while expanding far beyond basic budget cars.

— What makes BYD, Leapmotor and MG so interesting is that they are no longer competing on price alone. BYD now has the scale, technology and brand structure to compete from affordable city cars right through to luxury SUVs and record-breaking hypercars. Leapmotor is using Stellantis to accelerate its international growth, pairing aggressive pricing with REEV technology and a rapidly expanding model range. MG, meanwhile, has already proved that a Chinese-owned brand can become firmly established in Britain while still offering genuine value.

All three are taking different routes, but the direction is similar: more models, more local production, stronger technology and increasingly ambitious products. The result is a market that is moving faster, becoming more competitive and giving buyers far more choice than even a few years ago.

These are already important brands in 2026 — and their next phase of growth could make them far harder for established manufacturers to ignore. (Image Credits: BYD, Leapmotor and MG.)
