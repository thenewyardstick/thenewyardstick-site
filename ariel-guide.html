Ariel Guide

TNY 10 Sep 2026

Ariel is a small British manufacturer based in Crewkerne, Somerset, best known for stripping the sports car back to its essentials. Its modern reputation rests on the exposed-frame Atom, with the Nomad applying a similar approach to rougher ground.

Cars are built individually, and buyers work with the factory or specialist dealers to choose equipment suited to road, track or off-road use. These are specialist second cars, with limited weather protection and little conventional luggage space. The attraction is their directness and low weight, rather than luxury or everyday convenience.

Nomad 2 takes the lightweight sports-car idea somewhere muddier. Its exposed structure, generous suspension travel and purposeful tyres make it an unusual alternative to both a track toy and a conventional off-roader. It is for buyers who value an adventurous drive more than isolation from the elements. The short list of alternatives reflects that unusual mix: no mainstream model does quite the same job.

The Atom puts the driving experience on full display, from its exposed frame to the front wheels working beside you. It suits someone who wants road drives and track days to feel like events, and who can accept a helmet, limited luggage and a fairly involved arrival procedure. Choosing the right brakes, suspension and weather protection matters more than chasing the largest power figure. For those who do want the extreme, the limited-production Atom 4RR takes output to 525 bhp, although its £249,600 UK price puts it in an entirely different league from the regular Atom.
