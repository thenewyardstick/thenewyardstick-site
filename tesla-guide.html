Tesla Guide

Tesla is an American electric-car and energy company founded in 2003, and one of the most influential brands in the modern shift towards battery-powered transport. Its combination of long driving ranges, strong performance, over-the-air software updates and the Supercharger network helped push electric cars into the mainstream.


The brand has built its identity around simple, minimalist design and a highly digital ownership experience, with large central touchscreens replacing many conventional controls. Model 3 and Model Y form the core of the range, while larger and more specialised models sit above them. Tesla also continues to develop its battery, charging and autonomous-driving technology as part of a broader focus on electric transport and energy systems.

Model 3 suits drivers who want an efficient electric saloon with an uncluttered cabin and straightforward route planning. Its low seating position gives it a more car-like feel than the taller Model Y, while the large central screen handles much of daily life. Try those controls before committing. Long Range and Performance versions broaden the appeal beyond the relatively affordable starting model.

Model Y (22-)

Key rivals: Hyundai Ioniq 5, Kia EV6, Leapmotor C10, Peugeot E-5008, Volvo EX60

Model Y takes Tesla’s familiar screen-led approach and adds a wide-opening tailgate, a taller driving position and useful family space. It is the more practical choice for bulky luggage, pushchairs or regular rear-seat passengers. The latest update brings a more settled character, although equipment varies considerably between versions. Its integrated charging stops make longer electric journeys easier to organise.
