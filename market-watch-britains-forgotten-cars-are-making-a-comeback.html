Market Watch: Britain's Forgotten Cars Are Making a Comeback

📈 Market Watch 28 Oct 2026

As The New Yardstick reaches its 75th article and launches its Instagram account, we're taking a moment to look beyond new vehicle launches and future technologies.

While exploring Instagram's automotive community, we discovered a surprisingly enthusiastic following for the ordinary cars of the 1990s and 2000s. From dedicated owners to small enthusiast pages, there's considerable affection for models that were once an everyday sight on British roads.

And this enthusiasm extends well beyond social media. Hagerty's 2026 Festival of the Unexceptional attracted around 2,500 cars and more than 4,500 visitors, celebrating the everyday vehicles that rarely received much attention when new.

Many of these cars are gradually disappearing, yet their appeal seems to be growing. Some are becoming modern classics, while others remain remarkably affordable.


Once a familiar sight on British roads, the Citroën ZX has become one of the more overlooked family hatchbacks of the 1990s.

Our search uncovered a 1994 1.9D Avantage advertised for £1,995, with 122,000 miles, full service history and the same keeper for 20 years. Its sunroof and reportedly original towbar add period charm, although towing with just 71bhp must require some patience.

The naturally aspirated 1.9-litre diesel offers straightforward engineering and excellent economy, while the ZX's clever rear suspension made it surprisingly enjoyable to drive. Corrosion, rear-axle wear and timing-belt maintenance deserve attention.

Another 1.9D, a 1992 example showing 89,000 miles and some cosmetic wear, is advertised for £1,850. With so few available, we'd suggest a provisional £1,500–£2,500 shopping budget rather than a firm market average.

Turbo-diesel versions occasionally appear too, although the recent British adverts we located have since been removed.


The Ford Fusion arrived in 2002 with a straightforward idea: take the critically acclaimed Fiesta and give it a taller body, easier access and more luggage space. It offered many of the same mechanical strengths, but at a higher price and without quite the same charm.

Critics found its styling uninspiring and its ride surprisingly firm, while sales never approached those of its hatchback sibling. Ford subsequently introduced the B-Max, followed by the EcoSport, before the Puma finally made the raised, practical small Ford a mainstream favourite.

Today, the Fusion's lack of status is part of its appeal. We've found a 1.4 advertised for just £995 and a 1.6 automatic for £1,995, both with fewer than 80,000 miles.

Neither is a collectible rarity, but both offer inexpensive, practical transport. Buyers should check for corrosion, suspension wear and evidence of regular maintenance.

The Fusion may never become a celebrated classic, but does it deserve more recognition for anticipating the small crossover trend?


The Hyundai Matrix has always divided opinion, but time has arguably been kinder to its styling than expected. Its straight-edged profile, distinctive stepped window line and generous glass areas look surprisingly considered today, particularly knowing it was designed by Pininfarina.

Hyundai's transformation into a respected global manufacturer also makes its earlier, more unconventional models interesting to revisit.

Launched in 2001, the Matrix prioritised everyday practicality, offering a spacious interior, elevated seating position and generous accommodation within compact dimensions. Its straightforward 1.6-litre petrol engine remains a sensible choice, although buyers should inspect the underside for corrosion and check suspension wear.

Our search uncovered a 2006 1.6 manual for £995, a 2008 example with 84,000 miles for £1,250, and another 2007 manual for £1,450. Automatics are also plentiful, with examples advertised between approximately £1,000 and £2,200.

A future classic? Perhaps not conventionally, but the Matrix is an increasingly interesting reminder of Hyundai's earlier design ambitions.


Unlike many cars in this collection, the MG F was a genuine success when new. Launched in 1995, it became Britain's bestselling sports car for six consecutive years, with more than 40,000 UK sales. Yet today, the Mazda MX-5 arguably receives much greater recognition, while the MG F remains surprisingly affordable.

With its mid-mounted 1.8-litre engine, rear-wheel drive and distinctive Hydragas suspension, the MG offered an engaging alternative to conventional front-engined roadsters.

Our search uncovered a 1998 example with just 45,500 miles for £1,995, including recent cambelt and water-pump replacement. Other ordinary examples are advertised from approximately £1,100–£2,200. Buyers should investigate head-gasket history, suspension condition and corrosion.

The MG F also represents a very different chapter in MG's history. Today's brand concentrates on modern family cars, SUVs and electrification, although the all-electric Cyberster has finally revived its roadster tradition.

Perhaps it's time this once-popular British sports car received renewed appreciation.


The Mitsubishi Space Star was never a car to set pulses racing, but perhaps that's precisely why it deserves another look.

Sitting somewhere between a conventional hatchback and a small MPV, it offered generous interior space, comfortable seating and straightforward mechanicals, yet remained largely overlooked alongside more fashionable rivals.

Our search uncovered a particularly tidy 1.6-litre petrol example for £1,500. With just 54,000 miles, full service history, a year's MOT, sunroof, alloy wheels and air conditioning, it offers a surprising amount of everyday car for the money. The manual 1.6 was also capable of nearly 40mpg on the official combined cycle.

Elsewhere, we found a 1.6 automatic advertised for £999 and a manual for £1,740, suggesting a typical asking-price range of approximately £1,000–£2,000.

With Mitsubishi's reputation for durability, the Space Star represents affordable, practical motoring. It might never become a valuable classic, but perhaps it deserves recognition simply for being an honest, dependable family car.


The final Nissan Primera was an unconventional family car. Introduced in 2002, its unusual styling and centrally mounted instruments divided opinion, while the Toyota Avensis, Ford Mondeo and Vauxhall Vectra remained more familiar choices.

Available as a hatchback, saloon and estate, it also reminds us how much the market has changed. Nissan no longer offers a conventional saloon or estate in Britain, with its larger family models now dominated by SUVs and crossovers.

Beneath that distinctive exterior sat a spacious, comfortable and generally dependable car. Equipment was generous, with climate control standard and reversing cameras on many versions. The 1.8-litre petrol is a sensible choice, although buyers should check for oil consumption, timing-chain noises and electrical faults.

Our search uncovered a 2006 Primera 1.8 Flare manual with 70,841 miles for £995, while a 1.8 SE automatic was advertised for £1,499. Other examples suggest a realistic asking-price range of approximately £1,000–£2,000, excluding recorded insurance write-offs.

As affordable family transport, the overlooked P12 still makes considerable sense.


Launched in 2002, the Peugeot 307 SW offered something unusual: the practicality of a family estate with the option of seven seats. Competitively priced when new, with substantial dealer discounts adding to its appeal, it provided an affordable alternative to larger people carriers.

Its generous interior, panoramic glass roof and individually removable rear seats made it surprisingly versatile, although not every example included the optional sixth and seventh seats.

Buyers could choose from several petrol and HDi diesel engines. Today, the straightforward 1.6-litre petrol is arguably a safer choice than some later, more complex diesels, although the early 90bhp 2.0 HDi remains appealing. Electrical problems and general maintenance history deserve particular attention.

Our search uncovered 1.6 petrol examples around £995 and 2.0 HDi diesels from approximately £1,195. We also found seven-seat examples advertised for £850 and £1,850.

Overlooked and frequently dismissed, the 307 SW remains a remarkably practical family car for very little money.


Once an everyday sight on British roads, the original Renault Mégane is becoming increasingly difficult to find. Introduced in 1996, it replaced the Renault 19 and developed into a surprisingly extensive family, including a five-door hatchback, Classic saloon, Coupé, Cabriolet and the pioneering Scénic MPV.

An estate was also offered in mainland Europe, while the rugged four-wheel-drive Scénic RX4 later joined the extended family.

Today, it's the ordinary hatchback that feels particularly interesting. Its rounded styling has aged gracefully, while the 1.6-litre petrol remains a sensible choice. Cambelt history, cooling systems and ageing electrics deserve attention.

Our search uncovered a 1998 Mégane 1.6 RXE for £1,795, with just 42,000 miles and one owner from new. Garaged for 12 years, it has recently received a new cambelt, water pump, tyres and fluids. Another 2000 1.6 manual was advertised for £1,500.

Once commonplace, these cars are now becoming unusual simply because so few survive. Perhaps the unremarkable Mégane is finally becoming interesting.


The eighth-generation Toyota Corolla arrived in 1997 with unusually distinctive styling. Its rounded headlights earned it the nickname 'bug-eye', although buyers remained more interested in Toyota's reputation for reliability than its newfound character.

Available as a hatchback, liftback, saloon and estate, the Corolla offered comfortable, dependable family transport. The modest 1.3-litre petrol was never particularly quick, but its simplicity and durability were considerable strengths.

There's personal experience behind this choice. A T-registration 1.3 Corolla passed through three generations of our editor's family, proving itself comfortable, reliable and thoroughly unremarkable in the best possible way.

Our search uncovered a 1998 1.3 with just 37,476 miles for £1,200, alongside a 1999 example showing 53,000 miles for £1,295. Buyers should pay attention to corrosion, cambelt history and ageing suspension components.

While sporting Corollas attract increasing interest, these ordinary versions remain largely forgotten. Perhaps it's time the dependable everyday Corolla received some recognition.


Introduced in 2003, the Vauxhall Signum was an unusual attempt to turn the familiar Vectra into something approaching an executive car. With an extended wheelbase, generous rear legroom and sliding, reclining rear seats, it offered considerable comfort without the expense of a traditional luxury saloon.

An optional Travel Assistant even provided folding tables and a small fridge. Unfortunately, buyers struggled to understand where the Signum fitted into Vauxhall's range, and sales never really took off.

Our search uncovered a 2006 Signum 1.9 CDTi manual with 73,000 miles for £1,300, complete with a recently replaced clutch and flywheel. Another 2006 diesel automatic was advertised for £1,499, while a 2.2-litre petrol automatic appeared for £1,900.

Buyers should check diesel cambelt and water-pump history, inlet swirl flaps on the 150bhp CDTi, timing-chain condition on 2.2-litre petrol models and ageing electronics. The Signum never found its audience when new, but its unusual combination of space, comfort and affordability looks increasingly appealing today.

– These ten cars may never become highly valuable classics, but perhaps that's part of their appeal. They remind us of a time when ordinary family cars came in all shapes and sizes, often offering character, practicality and dependability without attracting much attention.

What began as an exploration of Instagram's automotive community has revealed something rather encouraging: these forgotten cars still have enthusiastic owners and admirers.

With a budget of £2,500, there are still opportunities to enjoy a little overlooked motoring history. Whether it's a dependable Corolla, an unusual Signum or a once-popular MG F, perhaps a car doesn't need to be particularly special to deserve being remembered.

Prices quoted are advertised UK asking prices identified during October 2026 and may change as vehicles are sold or listings are removed. Vehicle specifications, mileage, condition and history are based on advertised information and have not been independently verified. Buyers should undertake their own checks before purchasing.
