LA Motor Show 2026: TNY Explores the Biggest Reveals from Day One

✪ Featured 20 Nov 2026

Ford arrives in Los Angeles with a reminder that its performance heritage remains very much alive. The Mustang Dark Horse SC, powered by a supercharged 5.2-litre V8 producing 795hp, takes the familiar American muscle-car formula to an altogether more serious level.

In open-top form, it feels particularly at home in Southern California, where sunshine, coastal roads and the culture of performance motoring make it a fitting attraction for the LA Auto Show.

Yet Ford's future is not confined to powerful V8s. We had hoped to learn more about its forthcoming affordable electric pickup, the Fathom, but that story is still developing.

Camouflaged prototypes have been spotted testing extensively, and with its anticipated 2027 arrival approaching, we may not have long to wait for a clearer picture of Ford's next big move.


Dodge's new Charger Scat Pack takes a different approach to performance, retaining a traditional combustion engine while embracing a new generation of technology. Its 3.0-litre twin-turbocharged SIXPACK straight-six produces 550hp, making it a formidable performance car, although it sits below the 670hp all-electric Daytona Scat Pack.

For many American buyers, however, the petrol-powered version could prove the more appealing proposition. Familiar refuelling, an aggressive exhaust note and the ability to enjoy long-distance driving without planning charging stops all count for something.

The bigger question is whether Dodge will eventually bring V8 power back to the Charger. Reports suggest a supercharged HEMI could return for 2028, although this remains unconfirmed. For now, the SIXPACK is carrying the torch, but the V8 story may not be over.


Chrysler has a long history of moving American families, having helped establish the minivan as a mainstream family vehicle more than four decades ago. The Pacifica remains the centrepiece of that legacy, and its latest Copperline edition gives the familiar formula a sharper, more distinctive appearance.

Copper-tinted 20-inch wheels, contrasting exterior detailing and carefully chosen interior accents bring a welcome touch of personality, while the practical qualities that have made the Pacifica so popular remain intact. However, Chrysler faces a much bigger challenge than keeping its minivan fresh.

Following the departure of the 300, it has been reduced to a single-model manufacturer, leaving the Pacifica carrying the entire brand. Stellantis has already indicated that several new Chrysler models are planned by 2030, including an Airflow-related addition and further unnamed vehicles.

These future family-focused products could prove far more important than any individual Pacifica update, giving Chrysler the broader range it desperately needs to reclaim its place in the American market.


Cadillac is enjoying something of a renaissance, and its latest electric models are helping restore the American marque's sense of occasion. The VISTIQ is a particularly striking example, with sharp bodywork, dramatic vertical lighting and an imposing front end that shares the visual confidence of the much larger Escalade IQ.

There is even a hint of the extravagant ambition Cadillac has demonstrated with its six-figure Celestiq, a car intended to challenge the world's most prestigious luxury manufacturers.

Yet the VISTIQ takes that design philosophy into more familiar territory: a premium, three-row family SUV.

With seating for up to seven, 615hp from its dual-motor electric powertrain and an American starting price around $78,400 for the 2027 model, it offers considerable presence without requiring Rolls-Royce money. Cadillac is increasingly finding its own identity in the electric era.


Lucid is one of California's most ambitious automotive newcomers, and the Gravity GT-S provides a good illustration of where the company hopes to go next.

This sleek, seven-seat electric SUV combines an unusually elegant design with serious performance, its dual-motor powertrain producing 1,070hp and delivering a claimed 0–60mph time of just 3.1 seconds. It is a striking addition to the Los Angeles Auto Show, particularly given the company's ambition to establish itself as a major American luxury manufacturer.

However, Lucid's future depends on reaching beyond expensive flagship models such as the Air and Gravity.

Recent financial backing from Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund should help support its forthcoming, smaller electric models, including the Cosmos and Earth SUVs, which could eventually bring the brand to a wider audience. With European expansion also on the horizon, we hope a UK arrival will follow.


Although first unveiled in California in March 2024, the R2 feels particularly at home at the Los Angeles Auto Show. Now reaching customers, the smaller electric SUV represents an important step for Rivian as it looks beyond its more expensive R1S and R1T models.

More accessible in both size and price, the R2 retains the adventurous styling, practical interior and rugged character that have helped establish the brand. With more than 300 miles of estimated range in its launch specification, it offers the everyday usability expected of a modern family EV, alongside the freedom to venture further afield.

Rivian is increasingly positioning itself as a genuine challenger to Tesla, particularly the Model Y, but with a stronger emphasis on outdoor lifestyles and capability. The R2 could prove to be the model that takes Rivian from a premium EV specialist to a much broader American manufacturer.


From Rivian's American take on the electric family SUV, we move to Germany and Porsche's performance flagship: the Cayenne Turbo Electric. While Rivian is building its identity around accessibility and adventure, Porsche has taken the SUV concept to an altogether more extreme level.

With up to 1,140bhp available under launch control, the Cayenne delivers straight-line performance that only a handful of high-performance SUVs can rival. It can reach 62mph in just 2.5 seconds, figures that would have seemed extraordinary for a large family SUV only a few years ago.

Yet its styling is arguably just as interesting. The original Cayenne divided opinion with its bulky, sometimes awkward appearance; this all-electric version adopts a softer, more restrained design, retaining Porsche's familiar identity without quite the visual aggression of earlier models. It is a considerably more sophisticated-looking statement.


Hyundai is arguably one of the biggest players at this year's Los Angeles Auto Show, using the event to introduce its latest Elantra to North American audiences alongside a wider showcase of hybrid technology across the Hyundai Motor Group.

Already launched in South Korea as the Avante, the eighth-generation Elantra arrives with a much sharper interpretation of the compact saloon, adopting Hyundai's new 'Art of Steel' design language while growing in size to offer interior space approaching that of a larger family car.

The 2027 petrol model will be followed by a 2028 hybrid, pairing a 1.6-litre petrol engine with electric assistance and targeting up to 53mpg (US). The technology extends beyond Hyundai itself, with Kia and Genesis also introducing new hybrid options across their respective ranges.

It is a reminder that, while the group continues to invest heavily in electric vehicles, it sees considerable opportunity in improving the efficiency of familiar petrol-powered models too.


Kia's design team seems to be on a roll at the moment, and the latest Telluride is another strong example. Its upright proportions, distinctive lighting and confident stance give it plenty of road presence, while the redesigned interior moves the large family SUV further upmarket.

Although not officially offered in Britain, UK readers can think of it as a larger, more imposing relative of the Sorento, with dimensions closer to the all-electric EV9. The big news for 2027 is the arrival of the Telluride's first hybrid powertrain, pairing a 2.5-litre turbocharged petrol engine with electric motors to produce 329hp.

Kia estimates up to 35mpg (US) combined, while retaining seating for up to eight. It is a significant addition to the range, showing how Kia is bringing hybrid technology to even its largest family vehicles.


The G80 Hybrid is an important addition to the Genesis range, and one of the more interesting saloons on display in Los Angeles. Its elegant proportions, distinctive lighting and updated detailing give it plenty of presence, while the longer-wheelbase body places an even greater emphasis on rear-seat comfort.

Under the bonnet is a new 2.5-litre turbocharged hybrid powertrain, producing 347hp and 391lb-ft of torque, paired with a seven-speed automatic transmission. Genesis expects the system to improve fuel efficiency by around 30% compared with the conventional petrol G80, while delivering more than 500 miles of estimated range.

Although the conventional G80 is no longer offered new in the UK, Genesis continues to build its presence here, with its electric models forming the core of the current range.

The hybrid's arrival in America is a reminder that Hyundai Motor Group is taking different approaches to different markets, and has not abandoned the traditional large luxury saloon for customers who still want one.


If the G80 Hybrid demonstrates that Genesis still believes in the traditional luxury saloon, the GV80 Hybrid may be the more relevant proposition for European buyers.


Large premium SUVs remain hugely popular across the continent, and the GV80 has the imposing proportions and carefully judged styling to stand alongside established rivals. Making its North American debut in Los Angeles, the GV80 Hybrid shares its new 2.5-litre turbocharged hybrid powertrain with the G80 Hybrid, producing 347hp and 391lb-ft of torque.

Genesis expects a 24% improvement in fuel efficiency over the equivalent petrol model, with more than 500 miles of estimated range. The system also introduces a liquid-cooled hybrid battery, a first for Hyundai Motor Group.

Toyota's all-electric Highlander is an impressive addition to its American line-up, although it is unlikely to make the journey across the Atlantic. At almost 5.05 metres long, it is considerably larger than the electric Toyotas currently offered in Britain, with three rows of seating for up to seven and a distinctly family-focused brief.

The larger battery version targets up to 315 miles of range, while dual-motor models produce 338hp and offer all-wheel drive. There is a little history here for UK buyers: the previous Highlander Hybrid was briefly sold in Britain between 2021 and 2023, but never found the same audience as the considerably more popular RAV4 Hybrid.

Toyota eventually withdrew it, concentrating on higher-volume SUVs and leaving much of its premium SUV offering to Lexus. This new electric generation shows just how differently Toyota approaches the American market.


Acura has brought something rather special to Los Angeles with the NEXERA Vision, a dramatic two-seat sports coupé that looks every bit as striking as its name suggests.

Its low nose, muscular proportions, concealed lighting and butterfly doors make it a convincing showcase of what the brand's designers can achieve. However, this is more than simply an extravagant show car.

Developed at Acura's design studio in Los Angeles, the NEXERA introduces a new design language that will influence the next generation of production models, beginning with the RDX Hybrid, expected in 2028.

That means elements of its distinctive lighting signature and sculpted bodywork should eventually find their way onto much more familiar vehicles. The concept may never reach production in this form, but its influence is already planned, making it a meaningful glimpse into Acura's next chapter.


Not every interesting vehicle at the Los Angeles Auto Show has four wheels and an engine. Honda's Base Station is a lightweight, modular camping trailer designed to make family adventures more accessible, and it is arguably one of the more unusual ideas on display.

Developed by Honda's American design and engineering teams, it can be towed by relatively modest SUVs such as the CR-V. Its clever design allows the roof to raise, creating up to seven feet of standing room, while the interior can sleep four people.

Five large windows can be swapped for optional accessories, with a kitchen, external shower and air conditioning among the possibilities. Solar panels and an integrated battery provide off-grid power, too.

Making its first public appearance at the LA show, the Base Station demonstrates Honda's willingness to think beyond conventional vehicles and develop products around the outdoor lifestyles of its American customers.


The Nissan Rogue is effectively America's equivalent of our X-Trail, and this new 2027 model is particularly interesting because it previews the next generation of Nissan's family SUV. Making its public show debut in Los Angeles, the Rogue Hybrid introduces the latest evolution of Nissan's e-POWER technology to American buyers.

Its 1.5-litre turbocharged three-cylinder petrol engine acts solely as a generator, with electric motors driving the wheels – meaning it behaves much more like an electric car than a conventional hybrid.

The dual-motor, four-wheel-drive system produces 225hp, with a smoother, quieter and more responsive experience promised over its predecessor.

Our current X-Trail already uses e-POWER, but this latest system brings significant improvements and could offer a glimpse of the technology's future direction for Nissan's European family SUV.


The biggest takeaway from Los Angeles is just how varied the industry's future is becoming. Electrification continues to gather pace, but hybrid technology is also enjoying a resurgence, while traditional performance cars clearly retain an audience.

American manufacturers are developing vehicles around the scale and tastes of their home market, while international brands are increasingly tailoring their ranges to different regions. Not everything shown here will make it to Britain, but the decisions being made in California offer plenty of clues about what could be coming next.
