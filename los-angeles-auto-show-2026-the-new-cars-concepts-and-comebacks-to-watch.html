Los Angeles Auto Show 2026: The New Cars, Concepts and Comebacks to Watch

✪ Featured 10 Nov 2026

Ford heads to Los Angeles with one foot firmly in its performance heritage and the other in a very different future. The Mustang Dark Horse SC demonstrates that powerful combustion engines still have an audience, while the forthcoming Fathom could make electric pickups accessible to far more American buyers.

Confirmed for a 2027 launch, the Fathom is a five-seat midsize truck built on Ford’s new Universal Electric Vehicle Platform, with a planned starting price of $28,350. It will offer bidirectional power, allowing the vehicle to supply electricity to a suitably equipped home. Although Ford has revealed the name, technology and camouflaged prototypes, its finished design remains unseen.

TNY Design Studio has imagined how a production-ready Fathom might look, with a clean, purposeful front end and realistic pickup proportions. With Ford scheduled to address the media at LA, it is an especially relevant moment to consider what this affordable electric truck could become.


Ford's electric ambitions are only half the story in Los Angeles. The Mustang Dark Horse SC Convertible offers a striking counterpoint to the forthcoming Fathom, celebrating the power and theatre of traditional American muscle.

Powered by a supercharged 5.2-litre V8 producing 795 horsepower, it is the most powerful open-top Mustang Ford has ever built. A seven-speed dual-clutch transmission and specially tuned suspension combine formidable performance with the ability to enjoy longer journeys.

But there's more to this Mustang than its headline performance figures. California's open-top driving culture makes Los Angeles a natural stage for a Mustang that puts sound, performance and driver enjoyment at its centre.

With customer deliveries expected in spring 2027, the Dark Horse SC demonstrates that Ford's plans for affordable electric vehicles are developing alongside, rather than replacing, its enthusiast models.


Ford's plans extend far beyond the American vehicles appearing in Los Angeles. In Europe, the company is working with Renault Group on two affordable electric cars, the first expected in early 2028. Their shared underpinnings open up the possibility of a small Ford hatchback using the same platform family as the Renault 5 and Nissan Micra — and, potentially, the return of the Fiesta name.

Nothing has been confirmed about a Fiesta revival, and this is not a vehicle scheduled for LA. Nevertheless, the opportunity is certainly an interesting one. Ford withdrew the familiar Fiesta in 2023, leaving a gap in the supermini market just as affordable electric cars began gaining momentum.

TNY Design Studio's AmpR-based Fiesta rendering imagines how Ford might translate this new partnership into a recognisably Ford small hatchback, complementing the American-focused Fathom and Mustang stories.


Chevrolet is bringing one of America's most important pickups to Los Angeles: the completely redesigned 2027 Silverado 1500. Revealed earlier this year, the latest generation introduces more assertive styling, an extensively updated interior and considerably more technology.

Perhaps its biggest statement, however, lies beneath the bonnet. While much of the industry concentrates on electrification, Chevrolet has developed two entirely new V8 engines. The 5.7-litre produces 402 horsepower, while the larger 6.6-litre delivers 481 horsepower, reflecting the continued importance of conventional engines to American pickup buyers.

For Los Angeles, the Silverado provides an interesting contrast to the growing number of electric trucks. Rather than abandoning its established formula, Chevrolet is combining traditional V8 performance with modern driver assistance, digital technology and improved refinement.

It demonstrates that America's pickup market is evolving in several directions simultaneously, with familiar engineering remaining central to the industry's future.


With the sixth-generation Camaro gone, Chevrolet's performance-car range is now centred on the Corvette. But could Los Angeles offer the first clues about whether another, more accessible sports coupé might eventually join it?

Chevrolet has not announced a new Camaro, and there is no confirmed reveal scheduled for LA. Nevertheless, the show would be a fitting place to outline the next chapter for one of America's best-known muscle cars, particularly as Ford and Dodge continue developing high-performance models.

TNY Design Studio's hypothetical 2028 Camaro explores one possible direction. Our bright-yellow rendering combines familiar long-bonnet coupé proportions with a modern Chevrolet light signature, cleaner bodywork and a subtle ducktail spoiler, aiming for something that could plausibly reach production.

Whether its future involves V8 power, electrification or something between the two remains unknown, but the Camaro name is certainly one worth watching.


One of Cadillac's most exclusive recent performance models celebrates the brand's arrival in Formula 1 in 2026. The CT5-V Blackwing F1 Collector Series is limited to just 26 cars and carries a revised 6.2-litre supercharged V8 producing 685 horsepower. It also retains a six-speed manual gearbox, an increasingly rare feature in high-performance luxury cars.

Behind those impressive figures lies a broader ambition. Cadillac is expanding its electric luxury range while using motorsport to strengthen its reputation against established performance brands. The combination reflects a manufacturer keen to explore new technologies without abandoning its sporting heritage. Cadillac is confirmed for Los Angeles, although this particular edition has not been announced as a show exhibit.

For TNY, it also provides a useful starting point for our speculative 2029 CT5 rendering, exploring how Cadillac's sporting saloon could evolve while retaining its distinctive American character.


Cadillac has made an increasingly unusual commitment: the CT5 will return for another generation. While much of the luxury market has shifted towards SUVs and crossovers, General Motors is still investing in a traditional sporting saloon, with a successor expected later this decade.

TNY Design Studio's hypothetical 2029 CT5 explores what that decision could produce. Finished in dark emerald green, our rendering develops Cadillac's signature vertical lighting with a slim horizontal lightbar and a cleaner, Celestiq-inspired grille, while retaining the long, low proportions that distinguish the CT5 from the brand's growing SUV family.

At Los Angeles, the electric three-row VISTIQ is confirmed for display. Cadillac could also use the occasion to discuss its plans for future V-Series models or offer further clues about the CT5 successor. For now, the production design remains under wraps.


Buick's appearance in Los Angeles will centre on the Enclave, the largest model in its American range. In flagship Avenir specification, the three-row SUV combines an elaborate grille, 22-inch wheels, a technology-rich cabin and available Super Cruise hands-free driving. It reflects how far Buick has moved from its traditional image as a maker of comfortable saloons.

The organisers have confirmed the Enclave for display, but no all-new Buick unveiling has yet been announced. Rather than a dramatic concept launch, LA looks set to give American buyers a closer look at the marque's increasingly premium SUV offering.

There is, however, another side to Buick. In China, the company is developing an extensive Electra range, including modern electrified saloons. That contrast raises an interesting question for the American market: could Buick eventually find room for a saloon again?


Buick's Electra L7 offers a glimpse of a side of the marque American buyers rarely see. Built for China, the sleek electrified saloon has a sweeping roofline and carefully detailed lighting that sit comfortably alongside Buick's newer design language. It also raises a question: could similar ideas eventually return to US showrooms?

TNY Design Studio's hypothetical 2028 Electra explores that possibility. Our burnt-orange saloon shares a family resemblance with the L7, but imagines a distinct North American interpretation, with its own front-end treatment and road-ready detailing. The connection is stylistic rather than a suggestion that the Chinese-market L7 itself is heading to America.

Reports have suggested GM is considering a new Buick saloon for the United States, although nothing has been officially announced. With Buick appearing at LA, even a small hint about its plans beyond SUVs would be worth watching.


Chrysler begins Stellantis’ Los Angeles story from an unusual position: its 2027 range revolves around a single nameplate, the Pacifica. The former Voyager has been absorbed into the Pacifica LX line-up, leaving the minivan to represent one of America’s oldest automotive marques.

For 2027, the Pacifica's higher trim levels receive a striking new face, with vertical LED headlights, an illuminated 'piano-key' grille, updated badging and additional driver-assistance features. It retains the clever Stow ’n Go seating and available all-wheel drive that have helped establish its reputation with American families. Already unveiled in New York, the facelift gets another opportunity to reach buyers in Los Angeles.

But Chrysler’s ambitions extend beyond people carriers. Stellantis has outlined plans for three additional Chrysler crossovers, including a mid-sized model and two smaller vehicles drawing on European platforms.

That expansion provides an intriguing starting point for TNY Design Studio’s own interpretation of the Chrysler Airflow.


Chrysler first unveiled its Airflow electric crossover concept at CES in 2022, introducing a sleek design, distinctive LED lighting and an emphasis on connected technology. Four years later, the brand is finally preparing to expand beyond the Pacifica.

Stellantis has confirmed a new mid-sized Chrysler crossover using its flexible STLA One platform, which can accommodate different powertrains. Although the production name remains unconfirmed, an Airflow-inspired SUV could offer the space, comfort and technology expected of a modern American family car.

Our 2028 TNY Design Studio rendering explores how that original concept might evolve into a realistic production model.

Two smaller crossovers are also planned, using European-derived platforms potentially related to the Fiat Grizzly and Grizzly Fastback. These could provide affordable entry points to Chrysler's expanding range.

Los Angeles might offer further clues about all three models, their designs and when American buyers can expect them.


Moving from Chrysler to fellow Stellantis brand Jeep, Los Angeles is less about an entirely new model and more about celebrating one of America's most recognisable off-roaders.

The 2027 Wrangler Switchback is the final instalment in Jeep's Twelve 4 Twelve programme, a year of special editions marking the brand's 85th anniversary.

Based on the Wrangler Rubicon X, the Switchback combines contrasting black and grey body panels with bright Gecko-green accents, distinctive graphics and matching wheel details. Available with two or four doors, it also receives exclusive Nappa leather upholstery and contrasting interior trim.

The real attraction at Los Angeles will be seeing all twelve special-edition Wranglers displayed together for the first time.

Although Jeep has no entirely new model confirmed for a debut at LA, the Switchback offers a reminder of the Wrangler's enduring appeal, its customisation possibilities and Jeep's considerable off-road heritage.


Stellantis’ move from Jeep to Ram brings one of Los Angeles’ most interesting themes into focus: the V8 is attracting renewed attention.

After years of manufacturers downsizing engines and investing heavily in electrification, the 2027 Ram 1500 Rumble Bee makes a decidedly old-school statement.

There are three HEMI V8 options: a 395hp 5.7-litre, a 470hp 6.4-litre and a supercharged 6.2-litre producing 777hp in the Rumble Bee SRT. Ram quotes 0–60mph in just 3.4 seconds for the flagship, placing it firmly in sports-car territory despite its pickup body.

The Rumble Bee revives the American muscle-truck formula, combining dramatic styling and serious performance with everyday practicality.

Alongside Ford's supercharged Mustang Dark Horse SC, it suggests that enthusiasm for large petrol engines remains strong. It may not reverse the industry's shift towards hybrids and electric vehicles, but it certainly makes this year's Los Angeles line-up more varied.


Following Ram's return to V8 power, attention naturally turns to Dodge and the possibility of another major comeback.

The latest Charger already offers electric Daytona and petrol SIXPACK powertrains, both available in two-door coupé and four-door saloon body styles. The twin-turbocharged 3.0-litre SIXPACK produces 420hp in R/T form or 550hp in Scat Pack specification, while the electric Daytona Scat Pack delivers an impressive 670hp.

However, one ingredient many traditional Dodge enthusiasts still want is a HEMI V8. Recent reports suggest a supercharged 6.2-litre V8 Charger could arrive for the 2028 model year, potentially restoring the unmistakable sound and character of the Hellcat.

Our TNY Design Studio rendering explores how such a model might look. Los Angeles could provide further clues, although Dodge has yet to confirm a V8 return. If it happens, the Charger could offer one of the most varied performance-car line-ups in America.


After the 777hp Ram and talk of a V8 Charger, Stellantis offers something completely different at Los Angeles: the Fiat Topolino. Measuring barely 2.5 metres long, this two-seat electric runabout sits at the opposite extreme of traditional American motoring.

Already introduced in the US, the 2027 Topolino gains new colours, an Open Air version and road-legal low-speed vehicle capability. With a maximum speed of 19mph, it isn't intended for freeways, but could suit neighbourhoods, resorts and short coastal journeys.

Priced at around $14,000, it also offers an unusually affordable entry point to electric mobility.

Its appearance at LA raises an interesting question: could Americans embrace something genuinely small alongside their enormous pickups and SUVs?

That concludes our look at America's major automotive groups. From Ford's electric ambitions and GM's luxury and performance models to Stellantis' V8 muscle and tiny electric runabouts, Los Angeles promises an extraordinarily varied collection of vehicles.


Moving beyond America's domestic manufacturers, Volkswagen brings a distinctly different perspective to Los Angeles with the redesigned 2027 Atlas Cross Sport. Developed around North American tastes, the five-seat SUV combines generous dimensions with a lower, more coupé-like profile than the seven-seat Atlas.

An updated 2.0-litre turbocharged petrol engine now produces 282hp, paired with an eight-speed automatic transmission and optional 4MOTION all-wheel drive.

Inside, Volkswagen has pushed the Cross Sport further upmarket, offering Nappa leather, genuine wood trim, massaging front seats and a 15-inch touchscreen with AI-powered voice assistance. The Atlas Cross Sport illustrates how Volkswagen adapts its products to American buyers, offering the space and equipment expected in this competitive SUV market.

Already unveiled earlier this year, its appearance at Los Angeles provides an opportunity for a closer look, while Volkswagen's scheduled media presentation could bring further details ahead of its showroom arrival.


Moving from Europe to Japan, Toyota brings one of Los Angeles' most important new family SUVs: the fully electric 2028 Highlander.

For a nameplate traditionally associated with petrol and hybrid power, the transformation is substantial. The fifth-generation Highlander becomes Toyota's first three-row electric SUV for the American market, offering seating for up to seven.

Buyers can choose front- or all-wheel drive, with 77kWh and 95.8kWh battery options. The larger battery delivers up to 315 miles of manufacturer-estimated range, while all-wheel-drive versions produce 338hp. A 14-inch touchscreen, spacious interior and vehicle-to-load technology add to its family appeal.

Crucially, the Highlander will also become Toyota's first electric vehicle assembled in America, with production planned for Kentucky and batteries sourced domestically. For a manufacturer that has long championed hybrids, this represents a considerable commitment to electric family transport. Los Angeles provides an opportunity for buyers to examine it ahead of its anticipated 2027 arrival.


Acura brings a different kind of Japanese innovation to Los Angeles with the NEXERA Vision, a dramatic coupé concept created at its own California design studio.

First unveiled at Monterey Car Week in August, NEXERA combines a low, muscular stance with butterfly doors, active aerodynamics and ultra-slim 'phantom' lighting that appears through the bodywork when illuminated. Its driver-focused cabin mixes forged carbon with sustainable materials, but the real purpose of the concept goes beyond creating an eye-catching showpiece.

Acura says NEXERA establishes the design language for its next generation of production cars, starting with the fourth-generation RDX Hybrid arriving in 2028. That SUV will also introduce Acura's two-motor hybrid technology, making the transformation more than cosmetic.

For Los Angeles, the NEXERA's appearance is particularly appropriate. Designed in the city itself, it offers visitors an opportunity to examine the styling details destined to influence Acura's future models.


From Japan, our Los Angeles preview moves to South Korea and the completely redesigned 2027 Hyundai Tucson, one of the most important family SUVs in Hyundai's American line-up.

Already unveiled in October, the new Tucson arrives with Hyundai's angular Art of Steel design language, giving it a more substantial, upright appearance. Increased dimensions promise additional passenger and luggage space, while the interior introduces Pleos Connect, Hyundai's next-generation infotainment system with AI-assisted voice control.

The hybrid is particularly interesting, with Hyundai targeting up to 42mpg on the American combined cycle, although final certified figures remain to be confirmed. Competing directly with the Toyota RAV4 and Honda CR-V, the Tucson needs to deliver on practicality, technology and efficiency.

Los Angeles will give visitors a closer look at Hyundai's latest SUV, while the manufacturer has a separate global reveal scheduled for its 19 November press conference.


Staying with South Korea, Kia brings a very different proposition to Los Angeles: the 2027 Telluride Hybrid, a substantial three-row SUV designed with American families firmly in mind.

The second-generation Telluride actually made its debut at the 2025 LA Motor Show and is already on sale, making this year's appearance an opportunity to explore its first-ever hybrid powertrain.

Combining a turbocharged 2.5-litre petrol engine with electric assistance, it produces 329hp and 339lb ft of torque. Kia quotes up to 35mpg on the American combined cycle and an impressive potential driving range of 637 miles.

With bold, upright styling and a spacious interior, the Telluride demonstrates how hybrid technology can make larger family vehicles more economical without sacrificing performance.

Although not offered in Britain, it's an interesting example of how Kia is adapting its electrification strategy to American buyers.


To round off Hyundai Motor Group's Los Angeles presence, Genesis brings something considerably more extravagant: the GV90, its first full-size electric flagship SUV.

Unveiled in August 2026, the GV90 draws heavily from the striking Neolun concept, combining imposing proportions with an exceptionally luxurious interior. Available features include three-row seating, a retractable 24.6-inch OLED display and, on Neolun versions, dramatic coach-style doors with a concealed B-pillar.

Dual-motor versions offer up to 657hp, while American deliveries are expected to begin in early 2027.

The GV90 represents Genesis taking another ambitious step towards established luxury manufacturers, demonstrating that electric vehicles can offer considerably more than efficiency and performance.

From the family-focused Tucson and powerful Telluride Hybrid to the opulence of the GV90, Hyundai Motor Group's Los Angeles line-up illustrates just how far South Korean manufacturers have progressed — and how much further they intend to go.


We conclude our coverage of Asian manufacturers with one of Los Angeles' more unusual attractions: the FullGood Summer, a seven-seat plug-in hybrid from China with styling inspired by classic 1950s American vans. Its rounded bodywork, generous windows and distinctive proportions offer something refreshingly different from today's increasingly familiar SUV designs.

However, FullGood's appearance in Los Angeles is about considerably more than showcasing an interesting vehicle.

The Beijing-based manufacturer is making its American exhibition debut, with a dedicated press announcement on 19 November. It hopes to attract US investors, manufacturing partners and distributors, potentially allowing vehicles such as the Summer to be assembled and sold in America.

Nothing is guaranteed, but the company's ambitions are worth watching.

With no other Chinese passenger-car manufacturers currently confirmed for the show, FullGood provides an unexpected conclusion to our Asian coverage — combining nostalgic design, modern hybrid technology and genuine international ambition.

— If the 2026 Los Angeles Motor Show demonstrates anything, it is that the automotive industry no longer has one obvious direction. Powerful V8 engines are attracting renewed interest, electric vehicles are moving into increasingly varied segments, and hybrid technology is becoming central to everything from compact SUVs to large family vehicles.

American manufacturers are balancing familiar traditions with ambitious new products, while European and Asian brands continue adapting their offerings to one of the world's most influential car markets. There are also plenty of unanswered questions. Could Chevrolet revive the Camaro? How will Chrysler rebuild its range? And might new manufacturers such as FullGood eventually establish themselves in America?

Through our original TNY Design Studio renderings, we've explored some of those possibilities alongside the vehicles already confirmed.

With industry announcements scheduled for 19 November, Los Angeles could still deliver a few surprises. We'll be watching closely as speculation gives way to official news.
