What Does £1,500 Buy in 2026? Eight Used Cars We’d Look At

📈 Market Watch 28 Sep 2026

Cheap cars do not automatically have to mean bad cars. At the bottom end of the used market, £1,500 will inevitably involve compromises — higher mileage, older interiors and the odd cosmetic scar — but there are still plenty of genuinely useful, dependable cars available for the money.

The trick is looking beyond badge appeal and focusing on models that were simple, practical and affordable when new. Some offer huge amounts of space, others are exceptionally cheap to run, while a few give you levels of comfort or equipment that seem almost ridiculous at this price.


Dacia Logan MCV

The Dacia Logan has always been about doing a lot for very little, and that still makes it one of the strongest £1,500 buys for anyone who values space over style. The huge boot and roomy cabin make it genuinely useful, while simple mechanicals help keep running costs down.

Of the engines, the 1.5 dCi diesel suits the Logan best: it is economical, has enough torque to cope with heavier loads, and feels more relaxed when the car is fully loaded than the smaller petrol options.

At this budget, expect a 2014–2015 example with around 130,000 miles. It is basic, plain and short on luxury, but it covers the essentials well and offers an impressive amount of practical car for the money. (Image Credit: Dacia)


Fiat Panda

The Fiat Panda makes a particularly strong first car, combining low running costs with far more space and comfort than its tiny footprint suggests. Even on this budget, it is possible to find the newer-generation model, with most examples likely to be 2012–2013 cars showing around 100,000 miles.

The vast majority are petrol-powered, and that is no bad thing — the simple petrol engines are generally the safer, more dependable choice at this end of the market, while still being cheap to insure and economical to run.

Inside, the Panda is upright, airy and surprisingly practical, with enough room for everyday use without feeling cramped. 4x4 models command a clear premium, with usable examples typically starting closer to £2,500. (Image Credit: Fiat)


Honda Jazz

The Honda Jazz is another small car that makes a lot of sense at this end of the market. It is exceptionally roomy for its size, with Honda’s clever Magic Seats giving it a level of flexibility that many much larger hatchbacks cannot match.

Reliability is another major strength, making the Jazz especially appealing as a cheap everyday car. At around £1,500, buyers can usually choose between an early second-generation model with roughly 120,000 miles, or, if the boxier styling of the original Jazz does not bother them, a first-generation example with closer to 70,000 miles.

Either way, the Jazz offers a strong mix of space, simplicity and dependability without requiring a big budget. (Image Credit: Honda)


Renault Grand Modus

The Renault Grand Modus is one of the most overlooked cars at this price, yet its packaging is genuinely impressive. With the rear seats folded it offers up to 1,454 litres of luggage space, compared with 1,038 litres in a third-generation Clio, despite being only 48mm longer.

Its sliding rear bench can also trade rear legroom for extra boot space when required, adding even more flexibility. Engine choice is unusually broad too, including the turbocharged 1.2 TCe petrol and economical 1.5 dCi diesel, with the latter particularly well suited to heavier loads or regular longer journeys.

We found both petrol and diesel examples with around 100,000 miles comfortably within our £1,500 budget. (Image Credit: Renault)


Škoda Rapid

The Škoda Rapid is essentially the same car as the SEAT Toledo, but Škoda also offered the more versatile Spaceback, which sits somewhere between a conventional hatchback and a small estate. That makes it especially appealing as a cheap family car, with plenty of room without the bulk of a larger model.

While newer Škodas have moved much closer to Volkswagen in both price and positioning, the Rapid came from a simpler, more budget-focused era and now makes an excellent low-cost buy.

Most examples on the market are diesels, and around £1,500 will typically put you into a car with roughly 130,000 miles. It is not especially luxurious, but it is practical, straightforward and very good value. (Image Credit: Škoda)


Toyota Aygo

The Toyota Aygo was developed alongside the Peugeot 107 and Citroën C1, and all three are essentially the same car underneath. Most are powered by Toyota’s wonderfully simple 1.0-litre three-cylinder petrol engine, which has earned a strong reputation for reliability.

Despite its tiny capacity, it is not unusual to find examples with well over 200,000 miles, yet our £1,500 budget was enough to uncover earlier cars with around 60,000 miles. That leaves plenty of scope for cheap, trouble-free motoring if you buy carefully.

Real-world economy of around 60mpg is perfectly achievable too, helping keep everyday costs very low. That makes the extremely rare 54hp 1.4 diesel difficult to recommend unless you specifically want one. (Image Credit: Toyota)


Vauxhall Mokka

The Vauxhall Mokka may not be as stylish as the sleeker model that replaced it, but the original still delivers many of the traits that make SUVs so popular: a higher driving position, chunky styling and a more commanding view of the road. Better still, it now sits comfortably within the £1,500 budget, despite SUVs typically commanding a premium over equivalent hatchbacks.

The 1.4 Turbo offers a useful 140PS, giving the Mokka decent punch off the line while still being capable of around 40mpg in normal use. Our pick, though, would be the Isuzu-sourced 1.7 CDTi. It is economical, capable of around 55mpg, and has enough low-down torque to suit heavier loads or occasional towing.

Even four-wheel-drive versions can fall within budget, with roughly 100,000-mile petrols or 120,000-mile diesels appearing around the £1,500 mark. (Image Credit: Vauxhall)


Volvo S60

The Volvo S60 offers an impressive amount of car for £1,500. It still looks smart, the cabin feels far more upmarket than most alternatives at this price, and Volvo’s long-standing focus on safety adds another layer of appeal.

The diesel engines also suit the car well, combining strong motorway performance with respectable economy and plenty of torque. The trade-off is mileage: examples within our budget tend to start at around 150,000 miles, whether fitted with the smaller 1.6 diesel or the more powerful 2.0-litre unit.

That means condition and service history matter more than the number on the odometer, but a well-maintained S60 can still make a very convincing budget cruiser. (Image Credit: Volvo)

— At £1,500, condition matters far more than badge, age or mileage, and any purchase at this end of the market deserves a careful inspection and a look through its service history. But the examples above show that cheap does not have to mean disposable.


Whether you need something tiny and economical, a practical family car or even a comfortable long-distance cruiser, the examples above prove that £1,500 still buys far more than many people might expect. In some cases, it can even buy cars that offer space, comfort and versatility that would cost several times as much to replace today.
