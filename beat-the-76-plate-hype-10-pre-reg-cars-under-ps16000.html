Beat the 76-Plate Hype: 10 Pre-Reg Cars Under £16,000

📈 Market Watch 28 Aug 2026

With the new 76 registration arriving on 1 September, thousands of buyers will be preparing to collect a brand-new car. But having the latest number plate can come at a considerable cost. Today, the choice of new cars costing less than £16,000 is remarkably limited, with just three models starting below our budget: the Dacia Sandero, Dacia Spring and Leapmotor T03.

Hyundai i20 - Pre-Reg From: £15,990

The Ford Fiesta may have disappeared, but Hyundai still offers a very respectable answer to the traditional small hatchback. The i20 is a proper supermini rather than a city car, with five doors, a generous 352-litre boot and enough rear-seat space to make it a realistic option for a small family.

Its sharp styling has also aged well, giving the i20 considerably more presence than many budget-focused alternatives. Inside, the design is conventional and easy to understand, while Hyundai's five-year, unlimited-mileage warranty provides some additional reassurance for buyers planning to keep their car.

Kia Picanto - Pre-Reg From: £15,025

While it can't handle quite as many duties as its larger i20 cousin, the Picanto still has plenty going for it. Its compact dimensions and low running costs should particularly appeal to younger and urban drivers, while a tight 9.4-metre turning circle makes squeezing into small parking spaces and navigating busy streets considerably easier.

Kia's seven-year, 100,000-mile warranty is another major attraction, providing considerably longer cover than many rivals and making the Picanto an interesting prospect for buyers planning to keep their car.

Fiat 500e - Pre-Reg From: £14,999

Price cuts have already done the 500e a huge favour, while its recent qualification for the full £3,750 Electric Car Grant has made Fiat's once-expensive electric city car considerably easier to recommend. Interior quality is impressive for the money too, giving the 500e a genuinely premium feel that isn't usually associated with this end of the market.

The smaller-battery version offers 115 miles of range, making it best suited to urban use, while the longer-range model can manage up to 199 miles. With 220Nm of instantly available torque and 0-62mph taking just 9.0 seconds, performance is another unexpected advantage at this price.

Renault Clio - Pre-Reg From: £14,985

This is where pre-reg pricing starts to seriously disrupt the usual new-car hierarchy. For essentially the same money as a basic new Dacia Sandero, you can instead have its more upmarket Renault cousin. The Clio shares plenty of Renault Group thinking beneath the surface, but surrounds it with a noticeably more sophisticated package.

Even the affordable Generation trim includes a touchscreen infotainment system, smartphone connectivity and a digital instrument display, while the interior generally looks and feels considerably more expensive than its price suggests. There's useful space for four adults, a generous boot and the refinement expected from a mature supermini.

Dacia Sandero Stepway Extreme - Pre-Reg From: £15,885

Spend another £900, however, and Dacia comes fighting straight back. Rather than the basic Sandero we've used as our new-car benchmark, pre-reg money stretches to the more distinctive Stepway in high-spec Extreme trim, transforming the proposition considerably.

The raised ride height and chunky body cladding give it genuine crossover presence while providing some useful protection from everyday knocks and scrapes. Extreme models add distinctive Copper Brown detailing, black alloy wheels and a surprisingly upmarket-looking interior, while the large 10.1-inch touchscreen makes the smaller display fitted to our bargain Clio suddenly look rather modest.

Citroën C3 - Pre-Reg From: £14,990

Just as the Stepway appears to have reclaimed the value crown, Citroën comes along and steals it again. The reinvented C3 follows a remarkably similar philosophy, combining compact hatchback dimensions with chunky styling, a raised driving position and enough SUV-inspired personality to feel considerably more substantial than its price suggests.

A large 10.25-inch touchscreen brings plenty of technology to the cabin, while Citroën's emphasis on comfort arguably makes an even stronger impression. Soft suspension and supportive seats have helped make the C3 a new benchmark for affordable comfort, while Citroën's We Care programme offers warranty coverage for up to eight years or 100,000 miles.

Toyota Aygo X 1.0 - Pre-Reg From: £15,956

Toyota's smallest car has now gone hybrid, but remaining examples of the outgoing 1.0-litre petrol model shouldn't be overlooked. Its simple three-cylinder engine has developed an excellent reputation for reliability, while impressive real-world fuel economy makes the Aygo X particularly well suited to drivers wanting to keep everyday running costs low.

The boot might be one of the smallest in this company, but the potential warranty certainly isn't. Toyota's Relax programme can provide cover for up to 10 years or 100,000 miles, offering considerable peace of mind for anyone planning to keep their car long term.

Suzuki Swift - Pre-Reg From: £15,990

If you can get past the looks, there's an awful lot to like about the latest Suzuki Swift. Lightweight construction has long been one of its strengths, helping the little Suzuki feel nimble around town while also contributing to impressively low fuel consumption and running costs.

Its mild-hybrid petrol engine adds a little electrical assistance without requiring owners to plug anything in, making the Swift an uncomplicated option for buyers who simply want an economical small car. Suzuki also enjoys a strong reputation for reliability, while the Swift itself has become a household name after decades of providing affordable, lightweight transport.

Vauxhall Corsa - Pre-Reg From: £15,140

One of the biggest names in the supermini class, the Corsa remains a strong all-rounder. Its 100bhp 1.2-litre turbocharged petrol engine provides enough performance to avoid feeling underpowered, while the latest Vizor front end has transformed the design and given Vauxhall's small hatchback considerably more presence. A large touchscreen and familiar, straightforward interior add to its everyday appeal.

But here's where things get interesting. We've found pre-reg Corsa Electrics for just £120 more – roughly the price of two tanks of petrol. For that tiny premium, buyers get 134bhp, instant electric performance and up to 221 miles of range.

MG3 - Pre-Reg From: £13,495

And finally, the biggest bargain of the lot might leave more than £2,500 of our budget untouched. The MG3 is a proper five-door supermini, yet discounted examples can cost less than many considerably smaller city cars.

Its 1.5-litre petrol engine produces 114bhp, giving the MG3 enough performance to comfortably tackle faster roads and longer journeys where some low-powered city cars can begin to feel out of their depth. There's useful passenger and luggage space too, while its larger footprint gives it the road manners expected from a conventional supermini rather than something designed predominantly for urban use.

Fuel economy isn't class-leading and the MG3 isn't the most sophisticated car here, but that's missing the point. For buyers prioritising space, performance and sheer amount of car for their money, £13,495 is exceptionally difficult to argue with.

The New Yardstick Verdict

A new registration plate might look good on the driveway, but these deals demonstrate just how expensive that privilege can be. From established superminis and comfortable family runabouts to surprisingly capable EVs, pre-reg stock completely reshapes what a £16,000 budget can buy. If you're prepared to accept yesterday's registration number, the smart money may be sitting a little further back on the dealer forecourt.
