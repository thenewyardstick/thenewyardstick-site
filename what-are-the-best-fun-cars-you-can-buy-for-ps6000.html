What Are the Best Fun Cars You Can Buy for £6,000?

📈 Market Watch 7 Oct 2026

BMW 428i 

The BMW 428i is one of the most powerful cars in this list, and our £6,000 budget should be enough to secure a 2014 example with around 100,000 miles.

Its 2.0-litre turbocharged petrol engine produces 242bhp, giving the 4 Series plenty of pace without turning it into an outright M-car. Better still, the 428i is rear-wheel drive, giving it a layout that is becoming increasingly rare among affordable coupés. It also feels like a proper step up from a hot hatch, with a low driving position, long bonnet and a much more grown-up cabin.

At this age, condition and service history matter more than mileage, but find a well-kept example and the 428i offers a lot of performance, style and proper coupé character for the money.


Ford Fiesta ST

The Ford Fiesta ST is one of the most obvious choices here, but that is because it remains one of the best small performance cars of its era.

Our £6,000 budget can secure a 2013 example with fewer than 60,000 miles, giving buyers plenty of choice without having to accept huge mileage. Its 1.6-litre turbocharged petrol engine produces 180bhp, sent to the front wheels through a six-speed manual gearbox.

The figures are respectable, but the real appeal is the chassis: quick steering, strong grip and a playful balance that makes the Fiesta feel entertaining even at sensible speeds. It is also practical enough to use every day, which helps explain why the ST has remained such an enduring favourite with enthusiasts.


Infiniti Q30 

The Infiniti Q30 is closely related to the Mercedes A-Class, but for the same sort of money it offers something rarer, more distinctive and arguably even more upmarket inside.

Our budget should secure a 2015 example with around 100,000 miles, putting this unusual premium hatch firmly within reach. Infiniti gave the cabin softer materials and a more heavily trimmed feel, helping the Q30 come across more like a small luxury car than a conventional premium hatchback.

The 2.2-litre diesel produces 168bhp and 350Nm of torque, giving it strong mid-range pull and making overtaking feel effortless. Add in the unusual styling, comfortable ride and scarcity on UK roads, and the Q30 makes a surprisingly compelling alternative to the far more common A-Class.


MINI Paceman Cooper S ALL4

The MINI Paceman Cooper S ALL4 is one of the strangest cars in this list, but that is also a big part of its appeal. Our £6,000 budget should secure a 2013 example with around 80,000 miles, putting this unusual MINI comfortably within reach.

Effectively a three-door coupé version of the Countryman, it mixes raised suspension, four-wheel drive and distinctive styling with a 1.6-litre turbocharged petrol engine producing 181bhp. The ALL4 system gives it useful extra traction in poor weather, while the compact proportions and MINI underpinnings help it retain plenty of character.

Inside, you get the oversized switches and circular detailing typical of MINIs from this era. It is quirky, quick and far rarer than the regular hatchback.


Peugeot 308 GTi

The Peugeot 308 GTi is one of the biggest performance bargains here. Our £6,000 budget should secure a 2016 example with fewer than 100,000 miles, and for that you get a serious hot hatch with huge power from just 1.6 litres.

In its most potent form, the turbocharged petrol engine produces 266bhp, and with relatively little weight to haul around, that translates into seriously strong performance. It is also more economical than the performance suggests, with around 40mpg achievable on a sensible run.

With diesel prices currently so high, that makes the GTi a surprisingly appealing everyday option as well as an entertaining one. Add sharp handling, understated styling and genuine pace, and the 308 GTi looks remarkably complete for the money.


Renault Twingo GT

France’s hot-hatch formula gets shrunk to city-car scale with the Renault Twingo GT. Its tiny 0.9-litre turbocharged three-cylinder engine produces 108bhp, mounted beneath the boot and driving the rear wheels.

Our £6,000 budget should put a 2016 example with around 60,000 miles within reach. Its appeal comes from the unusual rear-engined, rear-wheel-drive layout, light weight, playful character and relatively low running costs.

It is also surprisingly practical, with five doors, a tiny turning circle and enough room for city-car duties. Few modern small cars are configured anything like it, making the GT one of the most interesting choices here.


Saab 9-5

The second-generation Saab 9-5 is the unicorn of this list. Sold for only a brief period before Saab disappeared, it is now an exceptionally rare sight on UK roads, which makes simply finding the right one part of the experience.

Patient shoppers may even see the 2.8-litre turbocharged V6 appear close to our £6,000 budget, bringing serious performance to Saab’s final big saloon. More commonly, the diesels make a strong case of their own. The 2.0 TiD offers good economy and long-distance comfort, while the punchier twin-turbo TTiD adds much stronger performance without abandoning diesel efficiency.

Whichever version you find, the 9-5 combines distinctive styling, excellent safety credentials and genuine rarity in a way almost nothing else at this price can match.


Vauxhall Adam S

The Vauxhall Adam S is a small hot hatch with far more character than its badge might suggest. Our £6,000 budget should secure a 2015 example with fewer than 60,000 miles, making it one of the younger and lower-mileage cars in this list.

Its 1.4-litre turbocharged petrol engine produces 148bhp, which is plenty in something this compact, while the short wheelbase and firm setup give it an eager, playful feel around town and on a twisty road.

It also makes a great alternative to the more obvious Abarth 500, offering similar pint-sized performance with a little more space and a much rarer presence on UK roads. Add distinctive styling and reasonable everyday usability, and the Adam S is easy to like.

— £6,000 still buys an impressive amount of character if you are willing to look beyond the obvious choices. From lightweight hot hatches and rear-wheel-drive coupés to rare premium oddballs and one of Saab’s final cars, there is plenty here to make everyday motoring more interesting.

As ever at this end of the market, condition and history matter more than chasing the lowest mileage, but the right example can deliver far more personality than many much newer cars.

Prices, specifications and availability were correct at the time of research and may have changed since publication.
