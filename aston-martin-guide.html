Aston Martin Guide

TNY 9 Sep 2026

Aston Martin is a British luxury manufacturer founded in 1913, best known for elegant sports cars and grand tourers. Its road-car business combines the long-established Gaydon operation with SUV production at St Athan in Wales, supported by specialist UK retailers.

The current range stretches from the two-seat Vantage to the more practical DBX, with the DB12 and V12 Vanquish occupying grand-touring territory. Valhalla adds a limited-production hybrid supercar. Personalisation is a significant part of the ownership proposition, so individual cars can cost considerably more than a basic list price. Convertible and S derivatives are grouped with their main model families here.

The Vantage is the compact, two-seat sports car of the Aston Martin family, with muscular styling and a more focused character than the larger grand tourers. The extensively updated car has a much more modern cabin than early examples. Roadster and sharper S versions broaden the choice, but the central appeal remains a front-engined V8 and rear-wheel drive.

The DB12 is Aston Martin’s elegant middle ground between sports car and long-distance grand tourer. Its cabin is a substantial step forward from the DB11, while the small rear seats provide occasional flexibility. Coupé, open-top Volante and more assertive S versions are grouped here. It suits buyers who want a special journey without choosing the most extreme Aston.

Valhalla moves Aston Martin into mid-engined hybrid-supercar territory, with a carbon-fibre structure and an emphasis on aerodynamic performance. It is a very different proposition from the brand’s front-engined grand tourers, aimed at collectors and committed enthusiasts. Production is limited, so a UK retailer’s ability to supply an allocation matters more than an ordinary showroom stock search.

The Vanquish is the big V12 statement in Aston Martin’s regular range. It combines a long, dramatic body with a two-seat cabin and a grand-touring brief, rather than trying to imitate a compact mid-engined supercar. The Volante adds open-air theatre. It is aimed at buyers who value the engine, craftsmanship and sense of occasion as much as outright pace.

The DBX gives Aston Martin buyers a genuinely useful family car, with five seats and a generous luggage area. The current 707 and S versions add formidable performance, while the revised cabin makes everyday use easier. Early used V8 models offer a much lower entry price. It suits owners who want the Aston character without giving up rear-seat space.
