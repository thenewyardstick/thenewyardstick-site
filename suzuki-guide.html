Suzuki Guide

TNY 17 Jul 2026

Suzuki continues to prove that bigger isn't always better. While its range is smaller than many rivals, every model focuses on low running costs, dependable engineering and everyday usability. Lightweight construction and efficient hybrid technology help deliver impressive fuel economy without unnecessary complexity, while the arrival of the all-electric e Vitara marks the beginning of Suzuki's next chapter. For buyers prioritising reliability, value and honest engineering over headline figures, Suzuki remains one of the smartest choices on the market.

Current Models

Suzuki Swift (24-)

RRP From : £19,999
Pre Reg From : c. £16,000
Used Price From : c. £14,000

Powertrains : MHEV
Dimensions : 3860 × 1735 × 1495 mm
Boot : 265L

Key rivals : Dacia Sandero, Fiat Grande Panda, MG3, Renault Clio, Toyota Yaris

The Swift remains one of the lightest and most enjoyable small cars on sale. Excellent fuel economy, surprisingly agile handling and generous standard equipment make it an appealing alternative to larger superminis. Although its bold styling and relatively modest boot won't suit every buyer, Suzuki has produced an efficient, dependable and refreshingly honest small car that continues to represent excellent value.

Suzuki Vitara (15-)

RRP From : £21,999
Pre Reg From : c. £19,000
Used Price From : c. £15,000

Powertrains : MHEV, Hybrid
Dimensions : 4185 × 1775 × 1610 mm
Boot : 362L, 289L Hybrid

Key rivals : Dacia Duster, Ford Puma, Kia Stonic, Seat Arona, Toyota Yaris Cross

The Vitara may be one of the longest-serving small SUVs on sale, but its lightweight construction, efficient hybrid powertrains and available ALLGRIP four-wheel drive continue to give it a unique place in the market. Competitive pricing and strong reliability make it easy to recommend.

Suzuki e Vitara (25-)

RRP From : £26,249
Pre Reg From : c. £20,000
Used Price From : c. £19,500

Powertrains : EV
Dimensions : 4275 × 1800 × 1635 mm
Boot : 306L

Key rivals : Jeep Avenger, Kia EV3, Renault 4, Vauxhall Frontera Electric, Volvo EX30

The e Vitara signals Suzuki's move into the electric era without abandoning the brand's traditional strengths. Compact dimensions, competitive pricing and available ALLGRIP-e four-wheel drive should make it an attractive option for buyers wanting a straightforward, dependable electric SUV rather than an overly complicated one.

Suzuki S-Cross (22-)

RRP From : £23,999
Pre Reg From : c. £19,500
Used Price From : c. £16,000

Powertrains : MHEV, Hybrid
Dimensions : 4300 × 1785 × 1580 mm
Boot : 430L, 293L Hybrid

Key rivals : Dacia Bigster, Hyundai Kona, Kia Niro, Nissan Qashqai, Peugeot 2008

The S-Cross focuses on practicality rather than fashion, offering generous interior space, efficient hybrid powertrains and the option of ALLGRIP four-wheel drive. It remains one of the most sensible family SUVs for buyers looking to keep ownership costs low.

Suzuki Across (20-)

RRP From : £49,679
Pre Reg From : c. £35,000
Used Price From : c. £19,000

Powertrains : PHEV
Dimensions : 4635 × 1855 × 1690 mm
Boot : 490L

Key rivals : Chery Tiggo 8, Hyundai Tucson PHEV, Kia Sportage PHEV, Peugeot 5008 PHEV, Toyota RAV4

The Across combines strong plug-in hybrid performance with excellent efficiency and genuine long-distance capability. It commands a premium new, but substantial pre-registered savings make it a far more compelling proposition for buyers wanting a spacious electrified SUV.

Specifications, dimensions and prices were correct at the time of the latest update. Recommended retail prices are official UK list prices, while pre-registered and used values are approximate UK market figures and may vary depending on specification, mileage, condition and location. Recommended retail prices include any applicable UK Government electric vehicle grant where available but exclude dealer discounts, manufacturer incentives and other retail offers.

Dimensions quote vehicle width excluding exterior mirrors unless otherwise stated. Boot capacities are measured or quoted up to the parcel shelf/load cover where applicable, rather than to the roof. For seven-seat vehicles, the listed boot capacity refers to space behind the third row with all seats in use unless otherwise stated.

Power outputs are quoted in bhp, with manufacturers' kW, PS or CV figures converted where necessary. Electric driving ranges use WLTP combined figures where available.
