Microcar Guide

TNY 7 Sep 2026

Microcars take the small-car idea back to basics, combining tiny dimensions with low weight and modest power for short everyday journeys. Most sit within the quadricycle categories rather than being conventional passenger cars.

L6e models are generally limited to 28mph and can be driven from age 16 with the appropriate licence, while faster and more powerful L7e models require a higher licence category and a minimum age of 17.

Their appeal lies in easy parking, low energy use and simple local mobility, although the roads and speeds involved make choosing the right category particularly important.

Commercial derivatives

Commercial microcars apply the same tiny dimensions and low running costs to short-distance deliveries and local business use. Cargo versions replace passenger space with dedicated storage, creating exceptionally compact alternatives to conventional vans. Their limited speed and range restrict their usefulness, but in congested towns they can offer a particularly space-efficient solution.

UK microcars may be approved as L6e light quadricycles or L7e heavy quadricycles rather than conventional passenger cars. L6e vehicles can generally be driven from age 16 with the appropriate AM licence entitlement, while L7e vehicles require the relevant higher licence entitlement and normally a minimum age of 17. Licence, insurance, registration and other legal requirements can vary according to the vehicle and driver; buyers should check current UK requirements before purchase.

Quadricycles are subject to different construction and approval requirements from conventional passenger cars and should not be assumed to provide the same level of crash protection. Safety equipment and crash-test performance vary considerably between models.
