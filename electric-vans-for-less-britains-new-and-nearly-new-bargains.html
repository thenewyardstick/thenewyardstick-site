Electric Vans for Less – Britain's New and Nearly-New Bargains

📈 Market Watch 11 Nov 2026
TNY MARKET WATCH
Citroën ë-C3 Van 44kWh Plus
Manufacturer RRP£17,550 + VAT
Brand-new price£13,250 + VAT
BRAND-NEW SAVING£4,300 (24.5%)

At just £13,250 plus VAT for a brand-new example, the Citroën ë-C3 Van makes a compelling case for businesses that rarely need a full-sized commercial vehicle.

Based on the passenger ë-C3, it replaces the rear seats with a compact load area, retaining two front seats, familiar car-like driving manners and Citroën's comfort-focused suspension.

Its 44kWh lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery offers approximately 200 miles of official WLTP range. LFP chemistry is particularly tolerant of regular charging to 100%, making more of that capacity available for everyday journeys without routinely limiting charging to 80%.

Another useful advantage is its towing capability. With a suitable approved towbar, the ë-C3 Van is rated to pull up to 550kg, potentially extending its appeal to businesses needing a small trailer. Its modest 300kg payload remains a limitation, but at this price, the little Citroën offers an appealing combination of affordability, versatility and low running costs.

TNY MARKET WATCH
DFSK EC35 39kWh 50 Auto SWB
Original manufacturer RRP£25,444 + VAT
Nearly-new price£6,995 + VAT
NEARLY-NEW SAVING£18,449 (72.5%)

Few vehicles in this comparison illustrate the unusual electric van market quite like the DFSK EC35. We found several 2024-registered examples advertised at just £6,995 plus VAT, some showing only 99 miles. Their registration dates and remarkably low mileages suggest vehicles that have seen little commercial use, although their histories should be checked individually.

Despite its modest price, the Chinese-built EC35 offers a genuinely useful 4.8m³ load area, twin sliding side doors and a claimed payload of 1,015kg. Its 38.7kWh battery provides a quoted WLTP range of up to 166 miles in the 50mph-limited version.

That speed restriction is the major compromise: this is a van for local deliveries, estates and short, predictable routes rather than regular motorway work.

Buyers should also establish battery health, warranty coverage and servicing support before purchasing. Nevertheless, the combination of commercial carrying capacity and near-delivery mileage at this price is difficult to ignore.

Ford E-Transit Courier Trend 46kWh Auto
Manufacturer RRP£27,000 + VAT
Brand-new price£16,989 + VAT
BRAND-NEW SAVING£10,011 (37.1%)

The Ford E-Transit Courier is one of the smartest-looking small commercial vehicles on sale, combining SUV-inspired styling with the practicality businesses expect from a Transit. Its compact footprint makes it easy to manoeuvre and park, yet the squared-off load area offers approximately 2.9m³ of space for tools, parcels or local deliveries.

For 2026, Ford increased usable battery capacity from 43kWh to 46kWh, lifting the official range to around 201 miles, depending on specification. Rapid charging from 10–80% can take approximately 26 minutes using a suitable DC charger, making it more flexible for businesses working beyond a fixed local route.

With brand-new examples advertised from £16,989 plus VAT, the updated van is particularly difficult to overlook when lightly used 43kWh versions can cost around £16,000. At this price, buying new offers the longer range and the reassurance of a fresh vehicle warranty.

TNY MARKET WATCH
Ford Transit City 300S 56kWh Auto L1 H1
Manufacturer RRP£29,000 + VAT
Brand-new price£18,250 + VAT
BRAND-NEW SAVING£10,750 (37.1%)

Ford's new Transit City brings an interesting proposition to the electric van market, offering substantial carrying capacity at a price normally associated with smaller commercial vehicles.

Positioned between the E-Transit Courier and E-Transit Custom, the compact L1 model provides approximately 6m³ of load space and an impressive 1,085kg payload. Its three-seat cabin, practical sliding side door and relatively compact exterior dimensions make it particularly appealing for urban deliveries and tradespeople.

A 56kWh LFP battery provides up to 158 miles of official WLTP range, while DC charging from 10–80% takes approximately 33 minutes. Although its range is modest compared with some alternatives, the Transit City prioritises everyday working capability and affordability.

With brand-new examples advertised at £18,250 plus VAT, representing a substantial reduction against the £29,000 list price, this is a particularly attractive option for businesses. Our example is advertised for delivery in December 2026.

TNY MARKET WATCH
Kia PV5 Cargo Essential 51.5kWh Standard Range L2 H1
Manufacturer RRP£27,645 + VAT
Brand-new price£21,495 + VAT
BRAND-NEW SAVING£6,150 (22.2%)

The Kia PV5 is perhaps the most futuristic-looking van in this comparison, with its clean, upright front end, distinctive lighting and unusually spacious body. Unlike a converted passenger car, it was designed from the outset as an electric commercial vehicle.

Our brand-new Standard Range Essential offer costs £21,495 plus VAT. Its 51.5kWh battery provides up to approximately 184 miles of WLTP range, while the boxy load area offers 4.4m³ of space, accommodating two Euro pallets. A low loading step makes everyday deliveries easier.

For businesses covering longer distances, the 71.2kWh Long Range Essential is also available from just £23,995 plus VAT. Its official range reaches 258 miles, providing an additional 74 miles for £2,500 more.

With modern technology, a seven-year vehicle warranty and eight-year battery cover, the PV5 looks particularly competitive for a purpose-built electric van.

Mercedes-Benz eSprinter 314 81kWh PRO Auto RWD L3 H2
Manufacturer RRP£58,850 + VAT
Nearly-new price£26,895 + VAT
NEARLY-NEW SAVING£31,955 (54.3%)

While significant discounts on smaller electric vans are becoming increasingly common, this Mercedes-Benz eSprinter stands out for its sheer size and capability.

The L3 H2 body offers an impressive 14m³ load area, paired with an 81kWh battery and rear-wheel drive. This is a full-sized commercial vehicle, suited to businesses transporting bulky goods rather than simply making short urban deliveries.

Our nearly-new 2025 example has covered just two miles, yet is advertised at £26,895 plus VAT, representing a saving of more than £31,000 against Mercedes-Benz's basic list price.

Even more remarkably, we've found a brand-new, unregistered example advertised at £27,855 plus VAT, just £960 more.

There are compromises, including an official range of approximately 161 miles and a relatively modest payload for such a large van. Nevertheless, these prices make the eSprinter a particularly compelling proposition for businesses considering electric transport.

TNY MARKET WATCH
Peugeot E-Expert 75kWh Professional Standard Auto
Manufacturer RRP£41,210 + VAT
Nearly-new price£15,995 + VAT
NEARLY-NEW SAVING£25,215 (61.2%)

Few electric vans demonstrate the scale of today's discounts quite like the Peugeot E-Expert. A familiar choice among commercial fleets, the Expert is widely represented on Britain's used market, with numerous remarkably low-mileage electric examples currently advertised.

Our facelifted 2024 Professional example has covered just 143 miles and costs £15,995 plus VAT. Its 75kWh battery offers an official WLTP range of approximately 194–230 miles, depending on configuration, while the Standard body provides 5.3m³ of load space and a payload of around one tonne.

The facelift also introduces a redesigned front end, a more contemporary dashboard and a 10-inch infotainment touchscreen.

Buyers prepared to accept the older design have even cheaper choices. Pre-facelift 2024 examples with fewer than 200 miles start around £14,490 plus VAT, while we found another with 2,000 miles for £13,790. With such an abundance of nearly-new stock, the E-Expert is particularly difficult to overlook.

TNY MARKET WATCH
Renault Master E-Tech MM35 87kWh Advance Auto L2 H2
Manufacturer RRP£42,500 + VAT
Brand-new price£25,795 + VAT
BRAND-NEW SAVING£16,705 (39.3%)

The latest Renault Master is a particularly interesting entry, combining a genuinely modern large-van design with a substantial discount. Its distinctive front end and carefully developed aerodynamics help the 87kWh E-Tech achieve an official range of around 255 miles in this MM35 configuration.

The updated cabin is equally appealing, featuring a 10-inch touchscreen, practical storage and a more contemporary dashboard than many traditional commercial vehicles.

This L2 H2 version offers 10.8m³ of load space. Although larger versions provide considerably more room, the medium-length Master strikes a useful balance between carrying capacity and manoeuvrability. Its payload of approximately one tonne is worth considering for heavier loads.

With brand-new examples advertised at £25,795 plus VAT, the electric Master is now priced surprisingly close to some conventional diesel alternatives. For businesses needing a spacious van with respectable range and modern technology, it makes a compelling proposition.

TNY MARKET WATCH
Vauxhall Combo Electric 2300 Prime 50kWh Auto L1 H1
Original manufacturer RRP£33,300 + VAT
Nearly-new price£11,995 + VAT
NEARLY-NEW SAVING£21,305 (64.0%)

The Vauxhall Combo is one of Britain's most familiar compact commercial vehicles, and its electric counterpart is becoming increasingly affordable. Our 2025-registered Prime example has covered just 3,353 miles, yet costs only £11,995 plus VAT.

This earlier 50kWh model offers up to 175 miles of official WLTP range. It misses out on the improved efficiency and potential 220-mile range of the newer 52kWh facelift, but remains well suited to predictable local journeys and overnight charging.

Despite its compact dimensions, the Combo Electric provides approximately 3.3m³ of load space and an indicative 803kg payload, making it useful for tradespeople and delivery businesses.

For those needing more room, we also found a long-wheelbase example with fewer than 100 miles advertised at £13,995 plus VAT.

With its established design, practical cabin and familiar driving experience, the Combo Electric offers considerable value for businesses willing to accept the shorter range.

TNY MARKET WATCH
Vauxhall Movano Electric 110kWh Prime Auto L3 H2
Original manufacturer RRP£50,385 + VAT
Nearly-new price£24,495 + VAT
NEARLY-NEW SAVING£25,890 (51.4%)

The Vauxhall Movano Electric rounds off our selection with one of the largest batteries available in this comparison. Our 2025-registered example has covered just 3,399 miles, yet is advertised at £24,495 plus VAT, less than half its original basic list price.

Its substantial 110kWh battery provides an official calculated combined range of approximately 235 miles in 3.5-tonne L3 H2 specification. The spacious 13m³ load area makes it suitable for bulky deliveries, removals and businesses requiring considerably more room than a compact van can offer.

The large battery limits payload to around 710kg in the 3.5-tonne version. However, the heavier 4.25-tonne Movano offers greater carrying capacity and can now be driven on a standard category B licence, thanks to recent changes in UK regulations.

With its modernised cabin, strong electric motor and significant discount, the Movano demonstrates just how accessible large electric commercial vehicles are becoming.

– The most striking finding isn't simply that electric vans are becoming cheaper, but how much choice buyers now have. From the £6,995 DFSK to heavily discounted large vans from Mercedes-Benz, Renault and Vauxhall, there are options for very different businesses and budgets.

Yet the lowest asking price isn't necessarily the best deal. Payload, driving range, charging access and the journeys a van makes every day remain important considerations. A local delivery business may find an ideal bargain here, while operators regularly covering longer distances or carrying heavy loads will need to choose more carefully.

What has changed is the starting point. With brand-new vans receiving substantial discounts and barely used examples appearing at a fraction of their original list prices, electric commercial vehicles deserve a fresh look from British businesses, including those that previously considered the switch too expensive.
