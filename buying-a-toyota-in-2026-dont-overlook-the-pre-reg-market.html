Buying a Toyota in 2026? Don't Overlook the Pre-Reg Market

📈 Market Watch 4 Nov 2026

At £23,995 new, the Toyota Yaris Icon Hybrid is hardly the bargain supermini it once was. But pre-registered examples from around £19,500 change the picture considerably, cutting almost £4,500 from the list price with little or no mileage accumulated.

Its 1.5-litre self-charging hybrid offers exceptional economy, with official combined figures exceeding 60mpg, while Toyota's established reputation for building durable models makes it an attractive long-term ownership proposition.

We have already explored the Yaris's impressive resale performance in our previous depreciation investigation, where comparable 2023 examples were retaining around 68% of their £20,500 pre-reg purchase price after three years, outperforming predictions.

Today's entry price is roughly £1,000 lower than the figure we used in that study.

For anyone prioritising low running costs, proven engineering and sensible depreciation, the Yaris is particularly compelling.


The Corolla Commercial is one of Toyota's more unusual offerings, transforming the familiar Corolla Touring Sports estate into a practical two-seat hybrid van.

We're finding pre-registered examples from £22,794 including VAT, representing a saving of almost £6,000 against Toyota's quoted new price.

Powered by Toyota's proven 1.8-litre self-charging hybrid, the Corolla Commercial delivers excellent fuel economy of up to 64.2mpg, while retaining the comfort, refinement and driving characteristics of a conventional estate car.

It's also a genuinely British-built product, assembled in Derbyshire using hybrid engines manufactured at Toyota's Deeside factory in North Wales.

With the rear seats replaced by a generous, separated load area, it offers considerable practicality without the running costs associated with many traditional vans. Provided you only need two seats, this could be one of the most sensible purchases in our selection.


The Toyota C-HR is already an attractive pre-registered proposition, with the stylish 1.8-litre Hybrid Design available from £27,990. However, it's the plug-in hybrid that really catches our attention.

At just £29,089, we're finding the 2.0-litre Plug-in Hybrid GR Sport for barely £1,100 more than the conventional hybrid.

With 223hp, 0–62mph in approximately 7.4 seconds and up to 41 miles of electric driving, it combines genuine performance with the potential for remarkably low running costs, particularly for owners able to charge at home.

The GR Sport's larger alloys and sportier styling also suit the C-HR's distinctive design particularly well. Perhaps most surprisingly, these examples undercut the pre-registered Prius plug-in hybrids we've found, which use a closely related powertrain, by over £8,000.

For buyers wanting something stylish, economical and surprisingly quick, this looks like exceptional value.


Toyota's first dedicated electric SUV has come a long way since its introduction, and pre-registered examples are becoming increasingly attractive. We're finding the well-equipped bZ4X Excel from £34,500 — almost £13,000 less than the £47,495 price of a new example, even after Toyota's current £1,500 manufacturer saving.

The updated model brings improved efficiency, stronger performance and a larger 73.1kWh battery. Front-wheel-drive Excel versions offer up to 319 miles of official range, while the considerably quicker all-wheel-drive model manages 292 miles.

Generous interior space, comfortable seating and a substantial equipment list make the bZ4X a convincing family SUV, with Toyota's reputation for reliability and extensive battery warranty providing additional reassurance.

Importantly, the revised bZ4X is a considerably more accomplished car than the original, particularly in terms of range and charging. At this price, it represents an impressive amount of electric family transport for the money.


The Hilux remains one of the benchmarks of the pick-up market, and Toyota's striking ninth-generation model makes a convincing case for staying at the top.

Its sharp new styling and range-topping Invincible X specification give this famously durable workhorse a more premium appearance, just as increasingly ambitious Chinese manufacturers enter the market.

We're finding pre-registered 2.8-litre Diesel 48V Invincible X examples from £31,100 plus VAT, equivalent to £37,320 including VAT. Against the £54,095 new-car price, that's a remarkable saving of almost £16,800.

Beneath the styling, the Hilux retains its rugged ladder-frame construction, four-wheel-drive capability, payload of up to one tonne and impressive 3.5-tonne towing capacity.
The mild-hybrid system brings improved refinement and efficiency without compromising its traditional strengths.

For buyers seeking a capable, generously equipped pick-up with a legendary reputation for durability, the Hilux remains extremely difficult to overlook.


Completing our selection is the Proace City Electric, with pre-registered Icon models appearing from just £16,690 excluding VAT. For a modern electric van carrying Toyota's badge, that's an intriguing starting point.

Although manufactured by Stellantis alongside equivalent Citroën, Peugeot, Fiat and Vauxhall models, the Toyota benefits from the brand's extensive dealer network and service-activated warranty programme.

Its recent facelift brings a sharper, more distinctive front end, while the modest 50kWh battery delivers up to 208 miles of official range — impressive efficiency for a compact commercial vehicle.

Despite its manageable exterior dimensions, the Proace City offers two body lengths, up to 4.4 cubic metres of load space using Toyota's Smart Cargo system and an electric payload of up to 800kg.

For tradespeople, local delivery firms and smaller businesses with predictable daily mileage, it combines low running costs, genuine practicality and Toyota's ownership reassurance in a particularly convincing package.

— Toyota rarely needs much introduction. Its reliability record, efficiency and sensible long-term ownership already makes it an attractive choice, but the pre-registered market strengthens that argument considerably.

Across these six vehicles, the opportunities are surprisingly varied. The Yaris offers economical hybrid motoring, the C-HR combines performance with electric flexibility, while the bZ4X and Hilux demonstrate just how substantial the discounts can become. Even Toyota's commercial range deserves a closer look.

Of course, pre-registered vehicles are technically used, even when they have barely covered any mileage. Buyers should check registration dates, warranty eligibility and servicing requirements before committing.

Nevertheless, when the specification suits your needs, paying thousands less for an almost-new Toyota could be one of the smartest ways into the brand.

Disclaimer: Prices and savings are based on UK-market research conducted in November 2026 and are subject to availability and change. Commercial vehicle prices may exclude VAT. Pre-registered vehicles have already been registered, meaning warranty periods may have commenced. Toyota's extended warranty cover is subject to qualifying servicing and eligibility. Manufacturer range and economy figures are based on official testing and may differ in real-world use.
