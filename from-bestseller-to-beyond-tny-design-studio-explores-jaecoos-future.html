From Bestseller to Beyond – TNY Design Studio Explores Jaecoo's Future

✎ Design Studio 23 Oct 2026

Jaecoo’s next move could be its smallest SUV yet. Reports suggest the Jaecoo 3 will measure around 4.2 metres long, putting it alongside rivals such as the Ford Puma and Renault 4.

The newcomer is reportedly being developed with European buyers in mind, with hybrid and fully electric versions both possible. A 2027 launch has been suggested, although Jaecoo has yet to confirm a date or release final specifications.

For TNY Design Studio, we’ve imagined how Jaecoo’s familiar styling might translate into a smaller SUV. Our rendering keeps the bold front grille and angular bodywork of the larger models, with compact proportions suited to British roads. It could become an interesting alternative in an increasingly competitive market.


The Jaecoo 5 offers a useful indication of where the smaller Jaecoo 3 might fit. Measuring 4,380mm long, it starts at £24,555 on the road, while the larger Jaecoo 7 measures 4,500mm and costs from £30,165 in petrol form.

Both share the upright, distinctly Range Rover-inspired styling that has helped Jaecoo attract attention in Britain, offering a premium appearance without premium-brand prices. At approximately 4.2 metres long, the forthcoming Jaecoo 3 would be 180mm shorter than the 5.

Based on the existing range, we estimate that an entry-level hybrid could start somewhere around £20,000–£22,000, with an electric version potentially costing more. That would place it among some fiercely competitive small SUVs, while retaining the design that has made its larger siblings so recognisable.


Jaecoo has proved that distinctive styling and competitive pricing can win over British buyers. Yet the small-SUV market is fiercely competitive, with established rivals offering plenty of choice. If the Jaecoo 3 is to follow the success of the 5 and 7, can it deliver enough to stand out?

At the opposite end of Jaecoo’s expanding range, something considerably larger could be on the horizon. The Jaecoo 8 has only recently arrived in Britain, bringing seven-seat practicality and a starting price of £45,500. Yet reports suggest an even more ambitious model is being prepared: the Jaecoo 9, a potential new flagship aimed at the luxury SUV market.

The likely foundation is Chery’s Fulwin T11, already available in China. Measuring 5,205mm long, it is 385mm longer than the Jaecoo 8, with six seats arranged in a 2+2+2 configuration. Its range-extender hybrid system uses electric motors alongside a 1.5-litre petrol engine that generates electricity.

A Jaecoo 9 would take the brand beyond the affordable premium SUVs that established its reputation, potentially challenging considerably more expensive machinery. However, its final British specification, pricing and arrival date remain unconfirmed.

For TNY Design Studio, we’ve explored how the imposing Fulwin T11 could be transformed into a Jaecoo flagship, retaining its luxurious proportions while introducing the brand’s familiar styling.


Pricing could become one of the Jaecoo 9’s biggest advantages. With the smaller Jaecoo 8 starting at £45,500, TNY estimates the 9 could cost approximately £55,000–£60,000 in Britain. That would comfortably undercut the fully electric Kia EV9, priced from around £66,000, and Volvo EX90, starting at £73,160.

Reports suggest the Jaecoo 9 is destined for Britain, although the manufacturer has yet to announce a confirmed European launch date, specification or price. Much will depend on whether Chery’s range-extender hybrid technology can be adapted for European regulations. If Jaecoo gets the pricing right, its largest SUV could attract considerable attention.


While the Jaecoo 9 could take the brand further into luxury territory, an equally important opportunity lies at the opposite end of the market. In April 2026, Chery’s head of engineering, Peter Matkin, revealed that the company had begun developing a new platform for vehicles measuring less than four metres long. The aim is to compete in a substantial part of the European market that Chery’s predominantly SUV-focused range has yet to reach.

Although the project is underway, no launch date, specifications or intended brands have been confirmed. There is currently no official Jaecoo 2, but the new platform raises an interesting possibility.

For TNY Design Studio, we’ve imagined a compact Jaecoo hatchback measuring approximately 3.9 metres, bringing the brand’s distinctive styling into Fiat Grande Panda and Renault 5 territory.

Together, a potential Jaecoo 2 and Jaecoo 9 would represent two very different ambitions: affordable urban motoring at one end and six-seat luxury at the other. Between them, the 3, 5, 7 and 8 could form a remarkably comprehensive SUV-led range.

Jaecoo's arrival in Britain has been remarkably successful, but maintaining that momentum will require more than repeating the formula that made the 7 a bestseller. The forthcoming 3 could introduce the brand to a wider audience, while the 9 would test its appeal among luxury SUV buyers. Further ahead, Chery's smaller vehicle platform could open an entirely new chapter.

Not every possibility explored here will necessarily reach production, but together they illustrate just how much room Jaecoo has to grow. The real challenge will be turning that ambition into cars that British buyers genuinely want.
