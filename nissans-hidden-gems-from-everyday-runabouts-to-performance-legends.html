Nissan's Hidden Gems – From Everyday Runabouts to Performance Legends

📈 Market Watch 19 Nov 2026

While the Nissan Micra is usually the obvious choice for anyone seeking an inexpensive small Nissan, its lesser-known sibling, the Pixo, makes an equally compelling case for bargain hunters.

Once Britain's cheapest new car, the Pixo was developed alongside the Suzuki Alto, sharing its straightforward mechanicals and economical 1.0-litre three-cylinder petrol engine. It isn't particularly exciting, spacious or luxurious, but that's hardly the point.

Simple engineering, proven Suzuki-sourced mechanicals and low running costs make it an appealing proposition. Real-world fuel economy of around 55mpg is achievable, too.

Best of all, examples with fewer than 90,000 miles can still be found for under £1,000, making the Pixo one of the cheapest routes into four-wheeled motoring.


Few small cars make quite the statement of the Nissan Cube. Its unusual, almost asymmetrical styling hides a surprisingly spacious five-seat cabin, with sliding rear seats and a light, airy interior.

Official UK models arrived with a 1.6-litre petrol engine, and examples with around 70,000 miles currently start at approximately £5,000. However, buyers have a much wider choice if they're prepared to consider Japanese imports, which commonly feature a 1.5-litre petrol engine and CVT automatic transmission.

These can be worth investigating, particularly for anyone wanting something a little different, but insurance should be checked before committing, as imported vehicles can attract different premiums. It's an unusual alternative to the conventional small family car.


Nissan was among the pioneers of affordable electric motoring, and the second-generation Leaf remains an appealing used buy. While standard models use a 40kWh battery, the more desirable e+ gets a larger 62kWh pack, delivering an official WLTP range of up to 239 miles.

It is a practical five-door family hatchback, with a 405-litre boot, decent performance and the benefit of genuinely inexpensive home charging. The technology isn't quite as modern as some newer rivals, particularly its CHAdeMO rapid-charging connection, but it remains a capable everyday electric car.

Prices for higher-capacity examples with around 60,000 miles start at approximately £9,000, making the e+ an increasingly tempting entry into longer-range EV ownership.


For buyers wanting something more substantial than the Leaf, the Ariya represents Nissan's next step in electric motoring. This stylish electric SUV offers a more upmarket cabin, generous passenger space and a comfortable driving experience, making it a convincing option.

Used examples now start at around £16,000, although we'd stretch the budget to approximately £19,000 for the larger 87kWh battery. Offering an official WLTP range of up to 329 miles in front-wheel-drive form, the long-range Ariya is particularly appealing for drivers who regularly cover longer distances.

With strong performance and an impressive standard of refinement, it feels like a considerable upgrade from the Leaf without requiring a luxury-car budget.


Not every bargain needs to be an obvious choice. The Nissan Pulsar is a largely forgotten family hatchback that never quite captured the British public's imagination, particularly when compared with the similarly sized Leaf electric car.

Yet the Pulsar offers plenty of practical appeal, with generous rear passenger space, a comfortable ride and a decent-sized boot. We'd seek out the 1.5-litre dCi diesel, which combines strong fuel economy with inexpensive road tax of just £20 a year on qualifying pre-April 2017 examples.

It isn't an exciting car, but it makes a convincing case as affordable everyday transport. With used examples starting below £2,000, the Pulsar proves that sometimes the most overlooked models offer the greatest value.


If the Leaf was Nissan's electric pioneer and the Pulsar its sensible family hatchback, the Infiniti Q30 was the premium alternative. Built at Nissan's Sunderland plant, the Q30 shares much of its engineering with the Mercedes-Benz A-Class, giving buyers an interesting combination of Japanese styling and German underpinnings.

There is plenty to like, particularly with the economical 1.5-litre diesel, which offers respectable performance and excellent potential for low running costs. Prices start at around £3,500, making it an intriguing alternative to the usual premium hatchbacks.

For buyers wanting something more spirited, the Q30 also offers a 1.6-litre turbocharged petrol, while the 2.2-litre diesel provides a stronger 168bhp and 350Nm.

This broader engine range gives it an advantage over the more budget-focused Pulsar, allowing buyers to choose between economy, performance and long-distance comfort. With its Mercedes connections and increasingly accessible used prices, the Q30 makes a compelling case as an affordable premium hatchback.


For buyers wanting something a little less predictable than the usual BMW 3 Series, the Infiniti Q50 makes a compelling alternative. Its sharp styling, rear-wheel-drive layout and high-quality, technology-filled cabin give it plenty of showroom appeal, even years after launch.

The 2.2-litre diesel is a particularly attractive entry point, producing 168bhp and a substantial 400Nm of torque, making it a punchy companion for longer journeys. Used examples can be found for under £4,000, representing considerable value for such a well-equipped car.

We'd also consider stretching the budget to around £7,000 for the 3.5-litre V6 hybrid. With 359bhp, it delivers serious performance while offering surprisingly respectable fuel economy for something with this much power. It's an increasingly rare sight on British roads, which only adds to its appeal.


For anyone who misses the days of big, naturally aspirated sports cars, the Nissan 370Z makes a very tempting proposition.

Its 3.7-litre V6 produces 323bhp, delivered to the rear wheels through either a manual or automatic gearbox. It's a proper driver's car, offering strong performance, an engaging driving experience and plenty of character without relying on turbochargers or electrification.

We're particularly drawn to the convertible, which adds open-top motoring to the package and, interestingly, appears to offer some of the best value in today's used market. Prices for 370Zs start at around £9,000. For a traditional V6 sports car, that's a lot of entertainment for the money.


If 323bhp from the 370Z isn't enough, Nissan's legendary GT-R takes things to another level. Its 3.8-litre twin-turbocharged V6 produces an impressive 478bhp in early UK models, rising to 562bhp in later versions, with power delivered through an advanced four-wheel-drive system and six-speed dual-clutch gearbox.

Capable of genuine supercar performance, the GT-R can accelerate from 0–62mph in around 3.5 seconds, yet it offers something many exotic rivals struggle to match: everyday usability.

There are four seats, a surprisingly useful 315-litre boot and enough refinement to make longer journeys perfectly possible. With early examples starting around £40,000, it's considerably more expensive than a 370Z, but offers an extraordinary amount of performance and engineering for the money.


After the excitement of the GT-R, it's time to return to reality with what could be Nissan's best all-rounder. The Qashqai has been one of Britain's biggest-selling family cars for years, and the third generation remains an appealing used buy.

We'd concentrate on the 1.3-litre mild-hybrid petrol, producing 138bhp and capable of returning over 40mpg in mixed driving. It's a sensible combination of performance and efficiency, wrapped in a practical, comfortable family SUV that still looks relatively fresh.

Prices start at around £7,500, while examples with approximately 70,000 miles can be found for under £10,000. Considering the third-generation model only arrived in 2021, that's impressive value for a modern family car.

Nissan's used-car range offers far more variety than you might expect, and there are bargains to be found at almost every budget. Whether it's a cheap Pixo, an electric Leaf, an overlooked Infiniti or the extraordinary GT-R, there's something here to suit very different needs.

The Qashqai remains the sensible all-round choice, but some of the less familiar models could prove even more rewarding. As ever, condition, service history and the right engine matter more than simply finding the cheapest example. Shop carefully, and Nissan's used-car catalogue has plenty to offer.
