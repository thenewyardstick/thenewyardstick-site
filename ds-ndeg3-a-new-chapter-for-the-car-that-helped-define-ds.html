DS N°3: A New Chapter for the Car That Helped Define DS

✎ Design Studio 18 Nov 2026

The DS 3 Crossback may have struggled to make the same impact as its predecessor, but there is still plenty to like about it. Its exterior styling remains distinctive, while the cabin is arguably its strongest feature, combining high-quality materials with quirky detailing and a design that stands apart from more conventional premium small SUVs.

It was also an important model for DS, becoming the first in the range to offer a fully electric version. Buyers could initially choose between petrol, diesel and electric power, before the UK range narrowed to hybrid and all-electric options.

With a current RRP starting at £33,385, the DS 3 is not especially cheap, but we have found pre-registered examples advertised for under £20,000. It still has plenty to offer; however, DS needs a truly standout model, and a return to the premium hatchback market looks to be its chosen direction.


Our interpretation of the DS N°3 builds on the F3VER concept first seen at the Paris Motor Show, translating its design cues into a smartly styled compact hatchback with a distinctly French character.

While its closely related Peugeot e-208 is expected to take a more technology-led approach, introducing steer-by-wire and a new generation of the i-Cockpit, the DS should place greater emphasis on premium materials, craftsmanship and comfort.

Sharing Stellantis' next-generation small-car architecture, the N°3 is expected to benefit from significant advances in electric performance and technology. An all-electric version is confirmed, although whether DS will also offer a hybrid powertrain remains unclear.

Either way, the challenge will be to create something that feels genuinely special, rather than simply a more expensive alternative to its Peugeot relation.


The original DS 3 was a genuine success, with approximately 445,000 examples sold in Europe, compared with around 91,000 of its SUV successor. Its appeal lay in its distinctive styling, huge choice of colour combinations, exceptionally efficient diesel engines and limited-run performance versions producing more than 200bhp.

However, the years since have seen DS become increasingly integrated with Citroën. What began as an attempt to establish a separate premium marque has evolved into a much closer operating relationship, with shared management, design leadership, platforms and components. Stellantis has now formally placed DS among its specialist brands, managed by Citroën, rather than treating it as an entirely independent operation.

This may make commercial sense, allowing DS to benefit from greater economies of scale, but it also raises questions about how distinctive the brand can become. With a new generation of models arriving, DS has an opportunity to establish a clearer identity and demonstrate that it can offer something genuinely different.


Our DS N°3 rendering offers one possible glimpse of where the brand could go next, but its wider transformation is already taking shape.

Two very different models are leading the way. The new DS N°7 replaces the DS 7, the brand's best-selling model, and looks a promising evolution. Its distinctive styling, upmarket interior and focus on comfort are complemented by a choice of efficient hybrid power or fully electric drive, with the long-range E-TENSE offering up to 460 miles on the WLTP cycle.

Meanwhile, the DS N°8 takes the brand in a more unusual direction. An all-electric flagship with a sleek SUV-coupé silhouette, it offers something rather different from the conventional premium SUV formula.

With its avant-garde design, impressive range and carefully crafted interior, the N°8 demonstrates that DS is prepared to explore new body styles rather than simply follow the established market. Together, they suggest a brand increasingly confident in its own identity.


Unveiled at the 2026 Paris Motor Show, the DS F3VER concept offered our clearest indication yet of the brand's future design direction. Although DS has not confirmed it as a direct preview of the production N°3, its compact proportions and hatchback-inspired silhouette make the connection difficult to ignore.

Measuring 4.19 metres long, it combines an unconventional, sculptural exterior with an emphasis on personalisation, including a customisable roof surround, extended two-tone paintwork and an evolving rear lighting signature.

DS also introduced its Design Intelligence philosophy, aiming to create greater visual impact through cleaner surfaces, fewer components and improved perceived quality. The concept is also intended to demonstrate how a compact car can deliver impressive electric range without sacrificing everyday practicality. It is an intriguing direction for a brand looking to rediscover its distinctive French character.



The next DS N°3 has an important role to play in the future of the brand. The original DS 3 proved that a small premium car could stand out through personality, clever personalisation and a little French flair, but its SUV successor never came close to matching that success.

With the N°7 and N°8 establishing a more distinctive direction at the larger end of the range, the opportunity now exists to bring that thinking into a smaller, more accessible package.

Stellantis provides the engineering, technology and economies of scale, but DS needs to offer more than familiar components wrapped in different styling. If the production N°3 can combine genuine individuality with quality, comfort and competitive electric range, it could become the standout model the brand has been missing. The challenge is considerable, but so is the opportunity.
