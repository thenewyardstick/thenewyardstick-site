An Estate for Every Budget – 12 of the Best from £2,000 to £20,000

📈 Market Watch 21 Oct 2026



Dacia Logan MCV Stepway

The Dacia Logan MCV earns its place here for one simple reason: extraordinary practicality for very little money. Its remarkable 573-litre boot beneath the parcel shelf expands to 1,518 litres with the rear seats folded. The load area is broad, square and refreshingly uncomplicated, making the Logan a compelling alternative to considerably more expensive family cars.

We've chosen the Stepway, although the cheapest Logan MCVs are conventional versions. The Stepway raises ground clearance by 50mm, while black wheelarch cladding, roof rails and more substantial bumpers give Dacia's deliberately simple design a surprisingly handsome, rugged appearance. It remains front-wheel-drive only, but that extra clearance is welcome on rough tracks and poorly maintained roads.

Higher-mileage Stepways can be found for less than £3,000, while a £5,000 budget opens up a wider selection of 0.9-litre turbocharged petrol and 1.5-litre diesel examples. Cars around 60,000 miles are worth hunting for, with both diesel and petrol models falling around the £5,000 mark.

For regularly carrying heavy loads, we'd favour the diesel. Its 220Nm of torque comfortably exceeds the petrol's 140Nm, and excellent fuel efficiency can help offset today's higher diesel prices, particularly over longer distances. The interior is basic and refinement modest, but few cars offer quite so much usable space for the money.


Škoda Fabia Estate

The Fabia Estate is arguably the best-value way into Škoda's famously practical estate-car range. It keeps everything refreshingly simple: a compact body, an uncomplicated interior and a genuinely impressive amount of luggage space. At just 4.26 metres long, it is easy to park and manoeuvre, yet offers a substantial 530-litre boot beneath the parcel shelf. Fold the rear seats and that expands to 1,395 litres.

Our preference is the 1.2 TSI petrol, particularly the 90PS and 110PS versions, which combine respectable performance with economical running costs. The four-cylinder engine also feels smoother and more relaxed than the later three-cylinder 1.0 TSI, particularly when the car is carrying passengers and luggage. Real-world economy around 48mpg is achievable, making the 1.2 a particularly appealing all-rounder.

Our £5,000 ceiling can secure a 2016–2017 example, with mileages generally starting around 90,000 at this price. The Fabia lacks the sheer carrying capacity of the Dacia Logan MCV, but compensates with a more polished driving experience, better-finished interior and compact dimensions.

For buyers who want a proper estate without the size or running costs of a larger car, the Fabia makes a remarkably convincing argument.


Fiat Tipo Station Wagon

The Fiat Tipo was something of a value champion when new, offering proper family-car space for considerably less money than its rivals. The Station Wagon originally started at just £13,995, and depreciation has only strengthened its appeal. Today, a £5,000 budget can stretch to a 2020 example, making the Tipo one of the newest estates at this price.

Practicality is its greatest strength. The generous 550-litre boot beneath the luggage cover is among the largest in its class, expanding to 1,650 litres with the rear seats folded. This creates a flat loading area capable of accommodating items up to 1.8 metres long. Passenger space is generous too, all wrapped up in an attractive, understated design that has aged rather well.

The 1.4 T-Jet turbocharged petrol offers lively performance, but real-world economy of around 40mpg is comfortably beaten by the 1.6-litre MultiJet diesel, which returns around 55mpg. With a substantial 320Nm of torque, it has the pulling power for heavy luggage, family holidays and towing, with a braked towing limit of up to 1,300kg on suitable versions.

Value becomes even more remarkable with earlier cars. 2017 Diesel estates covering around 100,000 miles can be found from £2,500, often with generous equipment levels. At these prices, the Tipo sets an exceptionally high benchmark for usable space per pound spent. For buyers covering longer distances, this could be one of the smartest purchases in the entire article.




Ford Mondeo Estate

The Ford Mondeo Estate is one of the great bargains of the used family car market. Large, comfortable and remarkably affordable, it delivers the sort of space and long-distance refinement that once made it a favourite with company drivers. Its generous cabin, supportive seats and composed motorway manners make it an excellent choice for families and anyone regularly covering longer journeys.

Practicality is equally impressive. The broad, accessible boot offers up to 525 litres beneath the luggage cover, expanding to 1,630 litres with the rear seats folded. There's plenty of room for passengers, while the long, usable loading area makes the Mondeo particularly well suited to bulky luggage and larger loads.

The 2.0 TDCi diesel is our preferred engine, with 150PS and 180PS versions delivering effortless motorway performance and real-world economy approaching 50mpg. Drivers covering shorter distances may find the 1.5 EcoBoost petrol a better fit. One important consideration is the later 2.0 EcoBlue diesel, introduced in 2019. This uses a wet timing-belt system, making correct oil specifications, documented servicing and adherence to Ford's replacement schedule important.

Our money would go on a well-equipped 2016–2017 2.0 TDCi. With £5,000–£8,000 securing attractive examples with fewer than 100,000 miles, the Mondeo offers an enormous amount of comfortable, practical motoring for the money.


Mercedes-Benz E-Class Estate

Few estate cars take load-lugging quite as seriously as the Mercedes-Benz E-Class. This generation was designed with practicality firmly in mind, combining enormous carrying capacity with the comfort, refinement and understated luxury expected of a Mercedes. Its sheer usefulness rivals that of many SUVs and MPVs, while remaining a particularly relaxing car for covering long motorway distances.

The boot is the real headline. In E250 CDI form, it offers a remarkable 695 litres beneath the luggage cover, some 135 litres more than the contemporary BMW 5 Series Touring. Fold the rear seats and capacity expands to 1,950 litres, extending that advantage to 280 litres. A wide opening, long load floor and self-levelling rear suspension further strengthen its credentials as a serious working estate.

The powerful 2.1-litre turbodiesel is an excellent match for the car, particularly the 204PS E250 CDI, which delivers 500Nm of torque. Unusually, Mercedes also offered the E300 BlueTEC Hybrid, combining a 2.1-litre diesel with electric assistance. It's an interesting alternative for economy-minded buyers, although boot capacity falls slightly to 650 litres.

£7,000–£10,000 secures attractive 2014–2015 examples with around 80,000–90,000 miles. We'd prioritise a comprehensive service history and excellent mechanical condition over optional extras. For buyers requiring carrying capacity without sacrificing comfort or a premium interior, this E-Class remains a compelling used purchase.


Peugeot 308 SW

The Peugeot 308 SW is one of the most convincing arguments for choosing an estate over an SUV. Its smart, understated exterior conceals exceptional practicality, while its manageable dimensions make it easy to live with. The 308 SW has also proved popular with British police forces, demonstrating its versatility as a working vehicle as well as a family car.

Practicality is a particular strength with 660 litres of boot space beneath the luggage cover, expanding to an impressive 1,775 litres with the rear seats folded. The low loading sill and flat-folding seats make excellent use of that space. Inside, the minimalist dashboard and large 9.7-inch touchscreen were remarkably forward-thinking for their time. Higher-specification GT models add sporty detailing, generous equipment and the option of a panoramic glass roof.

Our pick is the 2.0 BlueHDi GT automatic. Producing 180PS and 400Nm of torque, it combines strong performance with a smooth six-speed automatic gearbox, making it well suited to motorway journeys and heavier loads. Buyers prioritising fuel economy should consider the 1.6 BlueHDi, capable of around 60mpg in real-world conditions. The 1.2-litre turbo petrol is less convincing for frequent heavy loads or towing, and its wet timing belt requires attention to maintenance.

Remarkably, £6,000–£8,000 can secure a 2016–2017 2.0 BlueHDi GT automatic with approximately 50,000–80,000 miles. Considering its generous equipment, powerful drivetrain and enormous boot, the 308 SW represents outstanding value for buyers wanting something practical without sacrificing style or driving enjoyment.




Škoda Superb Estate

The Škoda Superb Estate has long been one of the most sensible large family cars on the market, combining executive-car comfort with exceptional practicality. What makes it particularly appealing today is the value. A new Superb Estate starts north of £38,000, yet our budget opens the door to the previous generation at a fraction of that price. It remains an impressive car, with generous equipment, strong build quality and a remarkably spacious interior.

The boot is among the largest in our entire selection, offering 660 litres beneath the luggage cover and an enormous 1,950 litres with the rear seats folded. The long, wide loading area makes transporting bulky items straightforward, while rear passengers enjoy exceptional legroom. 

The 2.0 TDI is our preferred engine, with the 150PS version offering an excellent balance of performance and economy. The more powerful 190PS diesel provides additional muscle for heavier loads and towing, while both suit the Superb's relaxed motorway character. TSI petrol engines can be perfectly pleasant for lighter duties, but fuel consumption is less appealing, especially when regularly carrying substantial loads.

Around £12,500 can secure a 2018 example with approximately 70,000–80,000 miles, although specification and service history influence prices considerably. We'd favour a well-maintained 2.0 TDI, checking the servicing record carefully, particularly on DSG automatics. For buyers wanting maximum space, comfort and long-distance ability without paying luxury-car money, the Superb is difficult to overlook.


Volkswagen Golf Estate

The Volkswagen Golf Estate offers an appealing middle ground between compact family cars and much larger load-luggers. At around 4.63 metres long, it's approximately 23cm shorter than the previous-generation Škoda Superb Estate, yet sacrifices surprisingly little luggage capacity.

The boot offers 611 litres with the rear seats upright, just 49 litres less than the Superb, expanding to an impressive 1,642 litres with them folded. A wide opening, adjustable boot floor and practical interior make excellent use of the available space. The eighth-generation Golf also brings a modern digital dashboard, generous safety equipment and a larger touchscreen than many similarly priced used estates.

Our preferred engine is the 2.0 TDI, available with 115PS or 150PS. It follows the same proven diesel formula as the larger Superb, delivering strong efficiency and relaxed motorway performance. The 150PS version is particularly appealing for regular heavy loads, although the 115PS diesel remains a sensible choice. The smaller 1.0 TSI petrol is adequate for lighter duties but feels less convincing when the estate is fully laden or towing.

A £12,000–£14,000 budget can secure a 2022 diesel estate with approximately 60,000–70,000 miles, while 2023 examples are worth watching at the upper end of that range. We'd prioritise service history and condition over trim level. For buyers wanting a relatively recent, economical estate with substantial carrying capacity but manageable exterior dimensions, the Golf makes an excellent case for itself.


Toyota Corolla Touring Sports

The Toyota Corolla Touring Sports brings something rather different to the estate car market: the space and practicality of a traditional family estate, combined with the remarkable efficiency of Toyota's self-charging hybrid system. The 1.8-litre petrol hybrid is our choice, delivering smooth automatic driving and excellent fuel economy without the need to plug in. Updated 2023 models also benefit from an increase in power to 140bhp.

Practicality remains impressive, with a generous 596-litre boot beneath the luggage cover, expanding to 1,606 litres with the rear seats folded. The loading area is wide and conveniently shaped, making the Corolla particularly useful for families and regular luggage carrying. Toyota even offers a two-seat Corolla Commercial van.

Efficiency is arguably the Corolla's greatest selling point, with around 60mpg achievable in everyday driving. Toyota's hybrid technology also has a reputation for longevity, with some Toyota hybrids exceeding 400,000 miles. While that is no guarantee for every example, a well-maintained Corolla makes a compelling long-term ownership proposition.

Our £13,000–£15,000 budget can secure a relatively recent 1.8 hybrid, with 2021–2022 examples offering plenty of choice with even 2023 examples appearing within this price bracket. With excellent economy, generous space and a proven hybrid pedigree, the Corolla is an estate designed to make financial sense for years to come.




Volvo V90

Few manufacturers have a stronger tradition of building estate cars than Volvo. From the famously practical estates of the past to the elegant V90, the Swedish manufacturer has long understood the appeal of combining generous space with comfort and understated luxury. Another familiar sight in British police fleets, the V90 is a particularly accomplished long-distance cruiser, with exceptionally comfortable seats, excellent refinement and a beautifully finished interior.

The V90 offers 560 litres of luggage space beneath the cover, expanding to 1,526 litres with the rear seats folded. Although its sleek roofline gives it a more elegant appearance than traditional Volvos, the loading area remains long, wide and genuinely useful. With almost two metres of load length available when the rear seats are folded, it is well suited to family holidays, bulky items and regular motorway journeys.

The 2.0-litre B4 petrol mild-hybrid produces 197bhp and offers a pleasing balance of performance and refinement, with around 37mpg achievable in everyday driving. Diesel versions offer better efficiency, potentially returning 50mpg, although they are relatively rare on the used market. Volvo ended diesel production in March 2024, bringing an end to an engine option particularly well suited to the V90's long-distance cruising abilities.

Around £18,000–£20,000 brings 2022 examples into consideration, including cars with approximately 50,000–60,000 miles. For those who value comfort, quality and effortless motorway performance, the V90 remains a wonderfully convincing alternative to buying an SUV.


BYD Seal 6 DM-i Touring 

The BYD Seal 6 DM-i Touring is perhaps the biggest surprise in our entire selection. Launched in Britain in late 2025, this is a genuinely modern plug-in hybrid estate, packed with technology and offering remarkable value. Buyers can secure an almost-new family car for considerably less than its original £34,990 asking price.

Practicality is impressive, with 500 litres of boot space beneath the luggage cover, expanding to 1,535 litres with the rear seats folded. There's generous passenger space too, while even the entry-level Boost includes a 12.8-inch touchscreen, reversing camera and powered tailgate.

The 1.5-litre plug-in hybrid combines petrol and electric power for smooth, economical driving. Boost models offer up to 31 miles of electric range, while the larger-battery Comfort stretches that to 62 miles. The petrol engine also makes longer journeys straightforward.

Our £20,000 budget can already secure a 2025 Boost with around 10,000 miles. Given its age, equipment and practicality, the Seal 6 represents extraordinary value, although its long-term reliability and resale performance remain largely unproven.


Vauxhall Astra Sports Tourer

The Volvo V90 and Škoda Superb demonstrate how easily estate cars can lose £20,000 through depreciation. The Vauxhall Astra Sports Tourer offers a clever alternative: buy one pre-registered for less than £20,000 and let the initial discount work in your favour. It's the only genuinely as-new estate available at this price.

The 130bhp 1.2-litre turbo petrol is our choice, with 2026 pre-registered examples advertised from just £18,995. Alternatively, £18,000–£20,000 can secure an all-electric 2025 model with around 5,000–10,000 miles. These can cover 256 miles of WLTP range, which is boosted to 275 miles in the newer version. Mild-hybrid and plug-in hybrid versions are also available, although they generally cost more when nearly new.

Despite its sleek styling, the Astra offers an impressive 597-litre boot, expanding to 1,634 litres with the rear seats folded. The electric version sacrifices some capacity to accommodate its battery, reducing those figures to 516 and 1,553 litres respectively.

Buyers seeking maximum practicality should also consider the closely related Peugeot 308 SW, which offers a slightly larger 608-litre boot and a more upmarket interior. However, the Peugeot generally costs more, meaning buyers may need to accept an older 2022–2025 example or higher mileage to remain within budget.



While traditional estates remain excellent all-rounders, buyers looking to maximise practicality should consider a few alternatives. These three models combine affordable running costs with clever, space-efficient designs, offering remarkable carrying capacity without the bulk or expense of a large SUV.


Citroën ë-Berlingo

Few vehicles demonstrate space-efficient design quite like the Citroën ë-Berlingo. Its upright, van-derived body maximises interior room without requiring the footprint of a large SUV. It's also a welcome example of British manufacturing, with electric Berlingos produced at Stellantis's Ellesmere Port factory in Cheshire.

The standard M version measures just 4.41 metres long yet offers an extraordinary 775-litre boot, expanding to 3,500 litres with the rear seats folded. The longer 4.76-metre XL can accommodate seven passengers, or provide 1,050 litres of luggage space in five-seat configuration. Fold the seats away and capacity stretches to an extraordinary 4,000 litres — more than twice that of the enormous Mercedes-Benz E-Class Estate.

The updated 52kWh battery gives the ë-Berlingo up to 213 miles of WLTP range, while its 136bhp motor provides smooth, effortless electric driving. Rapid charging at up to 100kW allows a 0–80% recharge in approximately 30 minutes. Range remains modest compared with larger electric cars, but affordable home charging makes everyday running costs particularly attractive.

The ë-Berlingo also offers impressive value when bought nearly new. Pre-registered examples are now available for less than £22,000, while low-mileage 2025 models are appearing from around £18,000, comfortably within our budget. For buyers prioritising maximum interior space, low running costs and modern electric motoring, few vehicles offer quite such a compelling package for the money.


Dacia Jogger Hybrid

The Dacia Jogger is one of the most versatile family cars available for the money. Combining the proportions of an estate with the flexibility of an MPV, it offers seven proper seats within a body measuring just 4.55 metres long. Its straightforward design prioritises usable space over expensive styling and unnecessary complexity.

With all seven seats occupied, boot capacity is a modest 160 litres. Fold the third row away and this increases to 565 litres, or 696 litres with those seats removed entirely. Maximum capacity reaches an impressive 1,807 litres with the second row folded, making the Jogger remarkably adaptable for family life.

Our choice is the 1.6-litre Hybrid 140. Its 140bhp output and automatic gearbox make easier work of carrying passengers and heavy luggage, while electric assistance helps reduce fuel consumption. Official economy reaches 56.5mpg, promising considerably lower fuel bills than many similarly spacious petrol-powered alternatives.

Around £16,000 can secure a 2023 Hybrid with approximately 30,000 miles, while £19,000 opens the door to a high-specification 2024 Extreme with around 10,000 miles. With seven-seat practicality, impressive efficiency and relatively modest running costs, the Jogger represents an enormous amount of family transport for the money.


Ford E-Tourneo Courier

The Ford E-Tourneo Courier proves that maximising interior space doesn't have to mean driving something enormous. At just 4.34 metres long, its upright proportions make excellent use of a compact footprint, while the angular styling and SUV-inspired front end give it a surprisingly sharp appearance for a van-derived family car.

Practicality is exceptional for its size. The five-seat Courier offers a generous 570-litre boot beneath the luggage cover, expanding to an impressive 2,162 litres with the rear seats folded and the space loaded to the roof. There's even an additional 44-litre compartment beneath the bonnet for charging cables and smaller items.

The earlier 43kWh electric model produces 136PS and offers up to 177 miles of WLTP range, making it particularly well suited to shorter journeys. The newer 46kWh version stretches that figure to 198 miles, although buyers wanting the additional range may need to increase their budget towards £23,000.

Early E-Tourneo Courier examples are now appearing below £20,000, making this an increasingly attractive alternative to a conventional estate. New examples also qualify for the full £3,750 UK Government Electric Car Grant, helping the Ford deliver strong value from new as well as used. For buyers prioritising space without unnecessary exterior bulk, it's a particularly clever package.

— What stands out most is just how much practicality the used market offers at every price point. From £2,500 diesels with enormous boots to nearly new plug-in hybrids and even pre-registered estates for less than £20,000, buyers have more choice than they might expect. And with some models offering close to 700 litres of luggage space, there's little reason to assume an SUV is the most practical option.

Whether your priority is fuel economy, long-distance comfort, seven seats or maximum carrying capacity, there's remarkable value to be found. The traditional estate remains one of motoring's most sensible ideas, and today's prices make the argument stronger than ever.
