Rolls-Royce Guide

TNY 10 Sep 2026

Rolls-Royce traces its origins to the partnership between Charles Rolls and Henry Royce in 1904. Today its cars are built at Goodwood in West Sussex, under BMW Group ownership, with an emphasis on quiet progress, craftsmanship and extensive personalisation.

The UK range spans the Cullinan SUV, electric Spectre coupe and the Ghost and Phantom saloons. Black Badge versions add a more assertive character, while extended-wheelbase saloons prioritise rear-seat space. Buyers commission cars through specialist retailers, so individual specifications can vary enormously. 

Current Models

Cullinan (18-)

Photo: Rolls-Royce Motor Cars

RRP From: £344,865
Pre Reg From: N/A
Used Price From: c. £180,000

Powertrains: Petrol
Dimensions: 5355 × 2000 × 1835 mm
Boot: 560L

Key rivals: Aston Martin DBX, Bentley Bentayga, Ferrari Purosangue, Range Rover SV, Mercedes-Maybach GLS

Cullinan puts the familiar Rolls-Royce sense of calm into a tall, practical SUV, but its real distinction is that it has no true direct rival. Its combination of immense presence, V12 power, rear-seat luxury and genuinely useful family space sits in a category almost entirely of its own. Buyers wanting something similarly luxurious usually have to move downmarket into less exclusive territory, while the only other genuinely exotic V12 SUV is the far sportier Ferrari Purosangue. Even then, the two serve such different purposes that a typical customer could quite reasonably own both.

Spectre (23-)

Photo: Rolls-Royce Motor Cars

RRP From: £333,185
Pre Reg From: N/A
Used Price From: c. £270,000

Powertrains: EV
Dimensions: 5490 × 2015 × 1575 mm
Boot: 380L

Key rivals: Aston Martin DB12, Bentley Continental GT, BMW i7, Ferrari Amalfi, Maserati GranTurismo Folgore

Spectre pairs the presence of a large luxury coupé with exceptionally quiet electric propulsion. It is designed to make progress feel effortless, with four proper seats and the opportunity to tailor almost every visible surface. The electric powertrain suits Rolls-Royce unusually well, adding near-silent acceleration to a car already built around refinement. It is for buyers who still want to drive themselves, but value comfort, atmosphere and effortless performance above sharp-edged excitement.

Ghost (20-)

Photo: Rolls-Royce Motor Cars

RRP From: £289,745
Pre Reg From: N/A
Used Price From: c. £165,000

Powertrains: Petrol
Dimensions: 5558 × 1998 × 1573 mm
Boot: 507L

Key rivals: Bentley Flying Spur, BMW 7-Series, Land Rover Range Rover SV, Mercedes-Maybach S-Class, Rolls-Royce Phantom

Ghost is the Rolls-Royce saloon for owners who are just as likely to take the wheel as settle into the back. It combines a discreetly modern cabin with relaxed V12 performance and remarkable attention to ride comfort. The Extended version favours rear passengers, while Black Badge adds attitude. All offer a highly personal alternative to the more familiar luxury saloons.

Phantom (17-)

Photo: Rolls-Royce Motor Cars

RRP From: £440,865
Pre Reg From: N/A
Used Price From: c. £220,000

Powertrains: Petrol
Dimensions: 5762 × 2018 × 1646 mm
Boot: 548L

Key rivals: Bentley Flying Spur Mulliner, BMW 7-Series, Land Rover Range Rover SV LWB, Mercedes-Maybach S-Class, Rolls-Royce Ghost Extended

Phantom is the most formal expression of Rolls-Royce luxury, with an imposing presence and a cabin designed to shut out the outside world. Its appeal lies in the quality of the experience rather than a list of gadgets. The Extended version gives rear passengers still more room. It is aimed at buyers seeking a particularly personal, unhurried and ceremonial way to travel.
