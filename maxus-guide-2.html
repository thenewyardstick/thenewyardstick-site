Maxus Guide

TNY 9 Sep 2026

MAXUS belongs to SAIC, the Chinese automotive group that also owns MG, while its UK history has roots in the former LDV commercial-vehicle business. Its passenger-car range takes a different direction from its traditional van business, focusing on large electric MPVs designed around space, comfort and multiple seating rows.

The range also includes diesel and electric pick-ups, adding towing, off-road and commercial capability alongside the MIFA people carriers. A much larger range of diesel and electric vans sits alongside them, giving MAXUS one of the broadest commercial-focused line-ups among newer Chinese brands in the UK.

The MIFA 7 gives families a proper people carrier at a time when most manufacturers would rather sell them an SUV. Sliding doors and separate second-row seats make loading people easier, especially in tight parking spaces. It suits buyers who regularly use all three rows, although luggage room behind the rearmost seats deserves a practical check before committing.

The MIFA 9 is the larger, more imposing Maxus people carrier, aimed at executive transport as well as big families. Its spacious cabin and available reclining second-row chairs put passenger comfort first. Seven- and eight-seat versions broaden its usefulness, but this is a substantial vehicle: check the driveway and usual parking spaces as carefully as the seating arrangement.

T60 MAX (25-)

Dimensions: 5395 × 1960 × 1870 mm

The T60 MAX gives MAXUS a conventional diesel option in the UK pick-up market, combining a double-cab body with useful towing and payload capability. Its strongest appeal is value, particularly against better-known rivals that can become expensive once similarly equipped. The cabin is more passenger-car-like than older work-focused pickups, while the standard four-wheel-drive layout keeps it suitable for towing and rural use.

eTERRON 9 (25-)

Photo: Maxus / SAIC

Dimensions: 5500 × 1997 × 1860 mm
Boot: Pick-up • Frunk: 236L

Key rivals: Ford Ranger PHEV, Isuzu D-Max EV, KGM Musso EV, Toyota Hilux BEV, Volkswagen Amarok

The eTERRON 9 is one of the few fully electric pickups currently available in the UK, combining a large battery with four-wheel drive and full 3,500 kg towing capability. It is significantly more expensive than the diesel T60 MAX, but brings stronger performance, quieter running and a useful front storage compartment. Its biggest compromise is payload, which is lower than most conventional diesel pickups.
