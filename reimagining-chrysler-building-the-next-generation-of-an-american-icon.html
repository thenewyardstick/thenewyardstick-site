Reimagining Chrysler: Building the Next Generation of an American Icon

✎ Design Studio 13 Nov 2026

Chrysler is still investing in its existing range. The 2027 Pacifica facelift introduces a striking new front end, illuminated wing badge, vertical LED lighting and updated interior details, demonstrating the brand's commitment to its current customers.

However, Stellantis' expansion plans point in a different direction. Rather than reviving the PT Cruiser or 300C, or developing a smaller Voyager, the focus is on a new family of SUVs. Historic nameplates, including Arrow and Airflow, offer a connection to Chrysler's past, while shared Stellantis platforms could make an expanded, more affordable range commercially viable.

The challenge now is creating vehicles that feel unmistakably Chrysler.


When Chrysler unveiled its Airflow concept in 2022, it offered a sleek, all-electric preview of the brand's future, alongside ambitious plans for an EV-only range. However, those plans have since changed.

After the original electric SUV programme was paused, a very different Airflow appeared briefly in a Stellantis presentation in June 2026. The newer design is more upright and practical, with sharper bodywork, distinctive vertical lighting and a full-width LED signature, all reflected in our TNY Design Studio rendering.

Expected for the 2028 model year, the new Airflow is expected to use Stellantis' flexible STLA One platform, supporting petrol, hybrid and fully electric powertrains. Early reports suggest petrol and hybrid versions could arrive first, replacing the original electric-only approach.

With pricing expected to start below $40,000, the Airflow could become an important foundation for Chrysler's expanding range.


Below the Airflow, Chrysler is planning two more affordable crossovers: the Arrow and Arrow Cross. Expected to start below $30,000, they could introduce the brand to a much broader audience, with both models anticipated around 2028.

Their foundations are already familiar in Europe. Based on the Fiat Grizzly family, both will use Stellantis' cost-effective Smart Car platform, shared with models including the Citroën C3 Aircross and Vauxhall Frontera.

The Arrow will adopt a sleeker coupé-SUV silhouette, while the Arrow Cross offers a more conventional, upright design. Petrol and mild-hybrid powertrains are expected to lead the American range, although final specifications remain unconfirmed.

Our Design Studio renderings explore how Chrysler's latest styling language could transform these affordable European foundations into something distinctly American, without the expense of developing entirely new vehicles.


The sleeker Chrysler Arrow could share its foundations with Fiat's new Grizzly Fastback, offering American buyers a more stylish alternative to the conventional SUV.

European models use Stellantis' familiar 1.2-litre turbocharged three-cylinder engine, available in petrol and mild-hybrid forms. However, American customers could be offered something different.

Stellantis has introduced a new-generation 1.6-litre turbocharged four-cylinder, manufactured in Michigan and powering the Jeep Cherokee's 210hp hybrid system. Could this engine, potentially in conventional petrol or hybrid form, find its way into Chrysler's smaller models?

Nothing has been confirmed, and adapting the engine to the Smart Car platform would require additional engineering. However, an American-built four-cylinder could offer stronger performance and greater appeal in the US market, while helping Chrysler establish a broader range of affordable vehicles.


Chrysler's future may ultimately depend on how successfully Stellantis can combine its global resources with the character and identity that made the American brand so recognisable.

The opportunity is certainly there. By sharing platforms, powertrains and components with established European models, Chrysler could introduce a broader, more competitive range without the enormous investment traditionally required to develop several new vehicles.

But affordability alone won't be enough. American buyers will expect distinctive styling, practical interiors, competitive performance and a convincing reason to choose Chrysler over increasingly capable rivals.

The Airflow, Arrow and Arrow Coupé imagined here at The New Yardstick Design Studio demonstrate how different segments could be brought together under a recognisable design language, while retaining their own personalities.

After years of contraction, Chrysler has an opportunity to become a more significant player in the American market once again. The next few years could determine whether that ambition becomes reality.
