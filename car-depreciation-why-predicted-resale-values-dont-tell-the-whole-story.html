Car Depreciation: Why Predicted Resale Values Don't Tell the Whole Story

📈 Market Watch 9 Sep 2026

Suzuki Ignis (2023)

RRP From: £17,349
Pre-Reg From: c.£16,500
3yr / 36k Predicted Value: c.51% / c.£8,850
3yr / 36k Actual Retail Value: c.£12,000 / c.69%
Value Retained vs Pre-Reg Price: c.73%

The Ignis demonstrates how affordable, distinctive cars can outperform conventional depreciation forecasts. A predicted three-year value of around £8,850 suggested buyers could expect to lose almost half of the original £17,349 list price. In reality, comparable 2023 cars are still retailing for around £12,000, retaining roughly 69% of their original value. Even against a more realistic pre-registered purchase price of around £16,500, that represents 73% retained. Limited supply, low running costs and the Ignis's unusual position as a tiny, lightweight crossover appear to have helped values remain remarkably resilient.


Fiat Panda (2023)

RRP From: £14,740
Pre-Reg From: c.£13,000
3yr / 36k Predicted Value: c.48% / c.£7,100
3yr / 36k Actual Retail Value: c.£9,000 / c.61%
Value Retained vs Pre-Reg Price: c.69%

The Panda tells a remarkably similar story to the Ignis. Its predicted three-year value of around £7,100 suggested a fairly typical depreciation curve, yet comparable 2023 cars are still retailing for around £9,000, retaining roughly 61% of their original list price and 69% of a typical pre-registered price. Much of that resilience comes from the Panda occupying an increasingly scarce position in the market: a genuinely small, affordable mild-hybrid city car with relatively few direct alternatives. With new city cars disappearing and demand remaining healthy, used buyers have fewer places to turn.


Toyota Yaris (2023)

RRP From: £22,125
Pre-Reg From: c.£20,500
3yr / 36k Predicted Value: c.57% / c.£12,600
3yr / 36k Actual Retail Value: c.£14,000 / c.63%
Value Retained vs Pre-Reg Price: c.68%

The Yaris was always expected to perform relatively well, but even its strong predicted residual value appears to have underestimated demand. Comparable 2023 cars are still retailing for around £14,000, retaining roughly 63% of their original list price and 68% of a typical pre-registered price. Toyota's reputation for reliability and the Yaris's efficient hybrid powertrain, low running costs and popularity with owners all help explain its resilience. Buyers should be careful when comparing cheaper advertisements, however, as the UK used market contains numerous imported Japanese-market Yaris models that can distort headline asking prices.


Vauxhall Corsa (2023)

RRP From: £18,585
Pre-Reg From: c.£15,000
3yr / 36k Predicted Value: c.41% / c.£7,600
3yr / 36k Actual Retail Value: c.£9,000 / c.48%
Value Retained vs Pre-Reg Price: c.60%

The Corsa also performed better than predicted, with comparable 2023 cars retaining around 48% of their original list price rather than the forecast 41%. However, the more interesting figure is what happens when substantial new-car discounts are taken into account. Pre-registered examples could be bought for around £15,000 rather than the £18,585 list price, meaning a car now retailing for approximately £9,000 has retained around 60% of that more realistic purchase price. It demonstrates why depreciation measured purely against RRP can exaggerate the amount of money an individual buyer actually loses.


Volkswagen ID.3 (2023)

RRP From: £37,115
Pre-Reg From: c.£32,000
3yr / 36k Predicted Value: c.52% / c.£19,300
3yr / 36k Actual Retail Value: c.£14,500 / c.39%
Value Retained vs Pre-Reg Price: c.45%

The ID.3 shows the opposite side of the story. Its predicted three-year value of around £19,300 suggested it would retain 52% of its original list price, yet comparable 2023 cars are now retailing for closer to £14,500, or just 39%. Even allowing for pre-registered discounts, that represents only around 45% retained. Electric cars have proved particularly difficult to forecast as prices, technology and demand have changed rapidly, while German models have not always delivered the residual-value strength traditionally expected of them. The ID.3 demonstrates how an apparently reassuring forecast can quickly become outdated.

Even against a typical pre-registered price of around £22,000, approximately 66% remains. A comfortable, well-built interior, strong standard equipment and an increasingly unusual position as a conventional family hatchback have helped make the Mazda 3 considerably more resilient than the original forecasts suggested.

More importantly, pre-registered examples could be bought for around £17,000, meaning roughly 65% of that more realistic purchase price remains today. Strong demand for small SUVs has undoubtedly helped, but the Juke demonstrates how buying at a substantial discount when new can completely change the depreciation calculation.


Dacia Jogger (2023)

RRP From: £17,165
Pre-Reg From: c.£16,000
3yr / 36k Predicted Value: c.55% / c.£9,440
3yr / 36k Actual Retail Value: c.£12,500 / c.73%
Value Retained vs Pre-Reg Price: c.78%

The Jogger is one of the clearest examples of the original predictions underestimating the market. Despite already starting at an unusually low £17,165, it was predicted to retain around £9,440 after three years. Comparable 2023 cars are instead retailing for approximately £12,500 — an impressive 73% of the original RRP and 78% of a typical pre-registered purchase price. Its combination of seven seats, enormous practicality and genuinely affordable pricing remains difficult to replicate, leaving used buyers with relatively few alternatives. Dacia didn't simply make the Jogger cheap; it gave it a niche that has proved remarkably resistant to depreciation.


MG HS (2023)

RRP From: £23,495
Pre-Reg From: c.£18,500

3yr / 36k Actual Retail Value: c.£12,000 / c.51%

The HS is another case where the original RRP tells only part of the story. Its predicted three-year value of around £10,810 represented 46% of list price, while comparable 2023 cars are actually retailing for around £12,000. More importantly, pre-registered examples could be bought for approximately £18,500. That means a buyer paying the discounted price has lost around £6,500 over three years — equivalent to just £181 per month in depreciation. For a spacious, well-equipped family SUV, that makes the HS's real-world ownership cost considerably more impressive than its predicted residual value suggested.

Against a typical pre-registered price of around £38,000, that rises to an impressive 71%. The Santa Fe's combination of genuine seven-seat practicality, generous equipment, strong hybrid options and increasingly premium feel has clearly translated into used-market desirability, demonstrating how far Hyundai has progressed from its old reputation as primarily a value-focused brand.

Instead, the Velar demonstrates how individual models can perform very differently, and why paying for a premium badge doesn't always guarantee a better return when it comes to resale.

Limited supply, enormous enthusiast demand and its rally-inspired engineering have made the GR Yaris far more than another hot hatch, with used values comfortably outperforming even the experts' optimistic expectations.

What Does This Tell Us About Depreciation?

The biggest lesson is that depreciation forecasts are useful guides, not guarantees. Across our 20 cars, some predictions proved remarkably pessimistic, while others underestimated losses by tens of thousands of pounds.

More importantly, RRP doesn't always represent what buyers actually paid. Pre-registered discounts can completely transform the ownership calculation, while scarcity, reliability, changing technology and simple used-car demand can push values in directions the experts never anticipated. The GR Yaris retaining around 90% while the Taycan 4S retains 46% demonstrates just how unpredictable the market can be.

The answer isn't to ignore depreciation forecasts — it's to never rely on them alone.
